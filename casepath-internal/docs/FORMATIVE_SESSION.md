# The first look: one session with the process owner

**Purpose.** Before five experts see CasePath, the person who drew Protekta's process works with it once. The question is not "is it good" but "does it work the way you work". What is alien gets changed in the days before the freeze.

**When.** Day 7 of the two-week plan, ninety minutes. A dry run of the same session takes place the day before, so the process owner never meets a bug we could have found ourselves.

**On which cases.** One synthetic practice case and two development dossiers, each as it stood at its decision point. Never the study cases (D2, D4, D7) or the reserve (D5): he will later label those blind.

## What must work by then

One development dossier, from start to finish, on casepath-pilot:
1. the case is prepared at its decision point, and the Übersicht opens;
2. every point under "Braucht Ihr Urteil" can be decided in place, with a reason in one tap;
3. changing a process step changes the documents, the letter and the next action at once, and says what changed;
4. the letter can be edited, copied, and the case closed ("erledigt" or "erledigt, mit Vorbehalt");
5. every statement opens its source; every action is recorded;
6. a note can be left on any screen or item with one click ("Anmerkung"), and is stored with what it refers to.

## The ninety minutes

| Minutes | What | What we learn |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **His way first, without CasePath.** One development dossier in the plain file view: "Show me how you would start on this case." He talks; we do not interrupt. | the order he works in, what he looks for first, the words he uses, what he writes down and where |
| 10 | The practice case in CasePath, with a two-minute introduction | whether the screen explains itself |
| 25 | **The same dossier in CasePath.** He handles it; he says aloud where it does something he would not. | where CasePath's order, words or conclusions differ from his |
| 20 | **A second dossier, alone,** no help unless he is stuck for a minute | what he can do unaided; where he gets stuck |
| 15 | **His process on the screen.** The process he drew next to what CasePath shows for this case: steps, names, the documents each step needs, the letter's form. | what is missing, misnamed or in the wrong place |
| 10 | Three questions: What would you stop using after a day? What would you miss if it were gone? What must change before your colleagues see it? | priorities, in his words |

## How what he says becomes change

Everything he does in CasePath is recorded like any expert's work. Everything he says is noted by the observer against the screen or item it concerns. After the session each point gets one class and one decision:

| Class | Example | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Wording | a label that is not Protekta's word | change it, always |
| Order | he looks at deadlines first, the screen shows them fourth | change it if it costs him a step |
| Missing | something he writes for every case has no place | add it if it belongs to the chain; otherwise note it for later |
| Wrong | a step, document or conclusion that is not right | fix it as a general rule, with its process step or legal basis |
| Preference | he would like it differently, colleagues may not | keep, and watch for it in the five sessions |

Changes are made on days 7 to 9 and only then is the system frozen. The list of what was changed, and what was deliberately not, goes into the study record.

## Two cautions

- He is also the one who will judge CasePath's statements on the study cases. So he gives this feedback on development cases only, and nothing he says about a specific case becomes a rule: only what holds for the process.
- One person's habits are not the unit's. Where his way and the documented process differ, we note both and let the five sessions show which the others follow.

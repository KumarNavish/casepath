This looks like a tenancy casework product that turns lessons from individual claims into reusable procedures and evidence checklists. These screens demonstrate how that knowledge develops, with a visible audit trail and checks before reuse.

What I can infer:

- **This is a recorded demonstration.** “Read-only,” “synthetic claims,” and the replay controls suggest I’m watching a prepared sequence, rather than triggering live learning.
- **Knowledge is organized into procedure families:** lease termination, rent increases, and defects and repairs.
- **Procedures have structured steps and dependencies.** The initial lease-termination procedure contains 11 steps, from capturing notice details through recording the outcome.
- **Claims can refine existing knowledge.** Version 2 adds a “proof of receipt evidence recipe,” links to the source claim, and identifies its parent version.
- **Reuse requires qualification.** The checklist covers rule compliance, branch combinations, source verification, consistency between process and checklist, matching evidence fields, and exclusion of personal case details. It reports 243 qualified regression cases.
- **Rule changes can affect multiple procedures.** A recorded requalification updates three families together.
- **The product distinguishes a reusable evidence recipe from evidence in a particular case.** The lease-contract card says the recipe was reused, but supporting evidence is still required for the current case.
- **Some proposed knowledge is withheld from reuse.** One candidate lacks a verified sufficient supporting file. “Refused” therefore appears to refer to rejected knowledge reuse, rather than a rejected legal claim.
- **Traceability is central.** Versions, timestamps, source claims, parent identifiers, receipts, and refusal details appear throughout.

My overall interpretation is that CasePath wants users to trust its learning by making each addition, reuse, revision, and refusal inspectable.

What remains unclear:

- **Who uses it and for what immediate task?** I can’t tell whether the primary user is a lawyer, caseworker, tenant, or knowledge reviewer.
- **What “learning” actually does.** It could be automated extraction, rule-based updates, human curation, or a combination. No reviewer or approval step is visible.
- **How learned knowledge changes work on a real case.** I see the knowledge history, but not the path from opening a claim to receiving useful guidance.
- **What several timeline markings mean.** The arrow icon and adjacent numbers need explanation. Likewise, `11 → 2`, `10 → 3`, and `11 → 6` could indicate changes in steps, rules, or something else.
- **What “qualified” guarantees.** The checks sound reassuring, but their coverage, author, and relationship to legal accuracy are unspecified.
- **Which law and jurisdiction apply**, and how the system handles outdated law or conflicting sources.
- **What the user should do about withheld knowledge.** The refusal is inspectable, but no correction or resubmission path is shown.
- **Why the refusal is called “Quarantine autotomy” followed by a long identifier.** That looks like internal terminology and obscures the otherwise understandable reason.
- **What distinguishes “Final view,” “History,” and the pinned procedure.** Their relationship is not apparent from these screens.

The clearest story is **versioned, source-backed procedural knowledge with controlled reuse**. The least clear part is how a user applies that knowledge to finish an actual case.

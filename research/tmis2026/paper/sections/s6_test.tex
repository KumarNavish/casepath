\section{Evaluation (Study 3)}
\label{sec:test}

We tested \casepath at a frozen commit against designs that give a model the same files and the same rules, on sealed synthetic data under a plan fixed in advance. We call this test Study~3. It has two data sets, theft and tenancy, each with its own workflow and measures. Section~\ref{sec:test-hypotheses} gives the registered outcomes. Sections~\ref{sec:test-cells} to~\ref{sec:test-lint} add the plan's registered secondary results and analyses added after the test. We mark the latter with a dagger (\dag) in headings, tables and figures; none is registered, none changes a hypothesis's status, and their $p$ values are descriptive.

\subsection{Data, designs and freeze}
\label{sec:test-data}

The test data, \texttt{fresh2/}, were built and sealed before any design ran on them (Supplement, Section~\ref{S-app:datasets}). Theft has \sthreeStories{} new stories: \sthreePairsChanged{} \emph{changed-fact pairs}, two texts that differ in one sentence that changes one of nine conditions, and \sthreePairsUnresolved{} \emph{unresolved-fact pairs}, in which the second text leaves that condition open. Tenancy has \sthreeClaims{} new claims in \sthreeFamilies{} \emph{families}; a family is four claims of one dispute type that share a combination of true facts. Half are in English and half in Swiss Standard German. Each tenancy dispute has a \emph{defining event}, such as receiving a written rent-increase notification. The plan defines three subsets from the reference: \sthreeClaimsDenied{} \emph{denied-event} claims, which say the event has not happened; \sthreeClaimsOpenEvent{} \emph{open-event} claims, which leave it open; and \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} \emph{ordinary} claims, of which \sthreeClaimsOpenBranch{} leave another fact open. The test set was designed to include the denied- and open-event claims.

The tenancy references were produced by the engine \casepath implements, from each claim's designed fact states. All designs except the two original-rules designs received the theft rules as reviewed (Section~\ref{sec:history}). The open rule (below) was set before the freeze; Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} records how. Table~\ref{tab:s3-timeline} gives the dates.

\label{sec:configuration}
All designs reached the models through OpenRouter's chat-completions API: gpt-6-luna, the \emph{cost-efficient model}, and gpt-6.1-terra, the \emph{frontier model}; no snapshot is recorded. Table~\ref{tab:s3-designs} lists the designs. \emph{Model executes rules} is the plan's design in the style of Chain of Logic \cite{servantez2024chain}: one prompt that states the rules' semantics and asks for each condition's value and the documents. \emph{Prior-work designs} are Self-consistency, Model executes rules and ReAct. The product ran with three readings, the open rule \texttt{any} (Table~\ref{tab:admission}) and a reasoning flag on, which sends a reasoning effort and no temperature. The registered tests ran the full product at the tested commit. Its agents outside the core (Supplement, Section~\ref{S-supp:pipeline}) cannot change a scored checklist. The core alone is the registered design \emph{\casepath, checklist only}, which skips those agents; it made \sthreeChecklistTenStrict{} strict unsafe errors per tenancy claim and \sthreeChecklistThErrors{} errors per theft pair, and the tables show both rows. On the frontier model the product ran once, in the checklist-only setting only. That setting skips the document judgments for a claim of one file; every theft text is such a claim, and theft scores requests only. A \emph{case-run} is one run of one design on one case: a \emph{claim-run} on tenancy, a \emph{text-run} on theft; a \emph{pair-run} covers both texts of a theft pair. The other designs ran \sthreeCasePathTenRuns{} times per case.

The measures are Study~2's. On tenancy, \emph{strict unsafe errors per claim} count five kinds of error: an \emph{unnecessary request} (a document the reference does not need), a \emph{premature request} (a document whose need depends on an open fact), a \emph{missed} document (needed and not requested), a \emph{handed-over} document (needed and passed to a person instead) and a \emph{silent skip} (a document whose need depends on an open fact, marked as not needed). \emph{All deviations per claim} add held documents that should have been requested and \emph{unneeded holds}, documents the reference neither requires nor holds. On theft, \emph{errors per pair} count the document changes between the two texts of a changed-fact pair that are wrong or missing. Unresolved-fact pairs are scored separately: a pair is \emph{unchanged} when the second text's checklist differs from the first only in the open condition's documents, and a \emph{premature request} requests one of the open condition's documents.

\subsection{Registered outcomes}
\label{sec:test-hypotheses}

Three of five hypotheses were confirmed, one failed and one was not tested (Table~\ref{tab:s3-hypotheses}); Section~\ref{sec:test-hold-prompt} adds a single prompt told the stop and hold rules, which the registered comparators were not. The plan tested them in a fixed order, each at the \sthreeAlpha{} level only if all before it were confirmed: H1 theft and H2 tenancy against Rules in context on the same model; H3 tenancy against Rules in context on the frontier model; H4 theft against each of Self-consistency, Model executes rules and ReAct, all three required; H5 theft non-inferiority to Rules in context on the frontier model, with a margin of \sthreeHFiveMargin{} errors per pair and a paired cluster-bootstrap test. H1 to H4 use exact two-sided sign-flip tests over clusters, the \sthreeTheftClusters{} changed conditions on theft and the \sthreeTenancyClusters{} families on tenancy. Beside H1, the plan's secondary contrast with the original rules does not depend on the comparator's rule base: \sthreeSecFourCA{} against \sthreeSecFourCB{} errors per pair ($p\sthreeSecFourCPeq$, registered secondary). On the frontier model, the plan's secondary non-inferiority contrast with the original rules (\sthreeSecFourBA{} against \sthreeSecFourBB) reports a bootstrap share of \sthreeSecFourBP, which is not a calibrated $p$; an exact sign-flip test for superiority, added after the test, gives $p=\sthreeFOneThVsRicOrigFrontierErrorsP$.

H4 failed against Self-consistency. Its exact test gives each of the nine cluster differences a random sign; its smallest attainable $p$ is \sthreeFThreeSCMinP, and it returned \sthreeFThreeSCP. \casepath had fewer errors on \sthreeFThreeSCFewer{} of the nine changed conditions and more on \sthreeFThreeSCMore, by \sthreePostSCExpertDiff{} and \sthreePostSCThirdPartyDiff{} errors per pair (expert procedure; third party caused the loss; Supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:s3-posthoc-sc}). \sthreeFThreeSCPatterns{} of the \sthreeFThreeSCAllPatterns{} sign patterns give a sum at least as far from zero. An interval from inverting this test, added after the test, runs from \sthreeRFiveHFourSCInvertedLo{} to \sthreeRFiveHFourSCInvertedHi{} and includes zero. With the eight stories as clusters instead, $p=\sthreeRFiveHFourSCStoriesP$, and H5's superiority $p$ is \sthreeRFiveHFiveStoriesP: the unit of clustering matters, and the registered unit stands. H5 was therefore not tested.

The plan reports the tenancy subsets beside H2 and H3 as descriptive family means. As the plan predicted, most of the tenancy difference lies in the \sthreeClaimsDeniedOpen{} denied- and open-event claims: \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenA{} strict unsafe errors per claim against \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenB{} and \sthreeHThreeDeniedOpenB{} for the two single prompts. On the \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} ordinary claims, \casepath made \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryA{} against \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryB{} for the same model ($p\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryPeq$, descriptive; the plan had predicted no difference) and \sthreeHThreeOrdinaryB{} for the frontier model ($p\sthreeHThreeOrdinaryPeq$). Supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:s3-subsets} gives the per-claim values. By family, added after the test, the effect sits in the \sthreeFFamGating{} families that contain denied- or open-event claims (termination and rent-increase disputes), where \casepath was lower in every family by at least \sthreeFFamGatingMinAbsRicFrontier{} errors per claim. In the other \sthreeFFamOther{} families (\sthreeFFamOtherDefect{} defect, \sthreeFFamOtherTermination{} termination) the differences are small and mixed: against Rules in context \casepath was lower in \sthreeFFamOtherFavourCasePathRic, equal in \sthreeFFamOtherEqualRic{} and higher in \sthreeFFamOtherFavourRic, by at most \sthreeFFamOtherMaxAbsRic{} (Supplement, Figure~\ref{S-fig:families}).

Under Holm's correction over the five hypotheses, which we did not register, H1, H2, H3 and H5 would pass and H4 would not. The registered outcome stands: three confirmed, one failed, one not tested.

An error decomposition, added after the test, attributes each of \casepath's errors to the stage that caused it (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). The run's stored state is replaced stage by stage, in a fixed order, by its designed value; the error that disappears at a stage is that stage's part, and what remains with every stage right is the rule's. The parts add up exactly, and in these data none of them changes with the order of the stages. A part can be negative where correcting one stage uncovers an error another had masked. On tenancy the rule part is zero by construction, because the reference comes from the engine; most of the cost-efficient product's errors are readings that admitted a wrong value, mainly a denied defining event read as having happened. On theft the rule part is \spostDecThLunaRule{} errors per pair in both cells, between \fuFloorLo{} and \fuFloorHi{} under two alternative mappings of designed conditions to rule facts. Supplement, Section~\ref{S-supp:decomposition} gives the single prompts' errors by kind.

\subsection{\dag\ Theft on the frontier model}
\label{sec:test-lint}

On theft, the decomposition puts the frontier model's extra errors, against the product on the cost-efficient model, in its readings and holds, and the stored readings trace them to two broadly worded conditions. The decomposition splits that cell's \spostDecThTerraMeasured{} errors per pair into \spostDecThTerraRule{} from the rules, \spostDecThTerraReading{} from readings and \spostDecThTerraHold{} from holds; the cost-efficient product has the same rule part and \spostDecThLunaReading{} and \spostDecThLunaHold{} for the other two (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). One condition requires a third party at fault. The frontier model admitted it as true in \fuLintGFortyTwoTerra{} of its \fuLintOffRunsTerra{} ordinary runs, on a sentence excluding any third party ``apart from the unidentified thief''; the cost-efficient model did so in \fuLintGFortyTwoLuna{} of its \fuLintOffRunsLuna{} ordinary runs, development runs included. The other concerns traces of the theft that were removed or altered; the frontier model's value for it changed between the two texts of a pair.

We propose a lint as a diagnostic for such conditions; it is a script written after the test and not part of the tested commit. It flags a condition that the readings admit as true in at least half of the runs on ordinary texts, or that at least one reading answers true in three quarters of them. The lint needs no reference answers, but its flags are many. Run on the theft test texts themselves, it flagged both conditions among \spostInSampleFlagsTerraN{} flags with the frontier model's readings (\spostInSampleFlagsLunaN{} with the cost-efficient model's). On the Study~3 tenancy claims it flags \spostTenLintLunaFlagged{} of \spostTenLintLunaConditions{} conditions with the cost-efficient model and \spostTenLintTerraFlagged{} of \spostTenLintTerraConditions{} with the frontier model, because those claims were built with several true branch facts each. In a mutation test on tenancy (one model, one run, USD~\spostMutSpendUSD), the analyst who designed the lint widened \spostMutPlanted{} conditions to general circumstances; all were flagged, but \spostMutPlantedAlready{} of them already were in their original wording, and \spostMutFalseAlarmMutated{} of \spostMutUnmutated{} untouched conditions were flagged too.

Treating the flagged conditions as open did not help out of sample (Supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:supp-lint}). With the frontier model's in-sample flags, its theft errors fell from \spostHeldTerraTestedErrors{} to \spostHeldTerraFlagOpenErrors{} per pair, at the price of a larger rule part and more held documents. With flags computed from development runs only, they fell to \spostHeldTerraDevFlagOpenErrors, still above the frontier prompt with the original rules; the development flags missed the traces condition. No paired difference is significant ($p$ from \spostPairedFlagOpenRicFrontierP{} to \spostPairedTestedRicFrontierP{} over nine clusters). Two blind AI editor sessions reviewed the flags under the four-eyes rule and could not agree on narrowing the third-party condition: one rejected it, because its source passage states no exclusion.

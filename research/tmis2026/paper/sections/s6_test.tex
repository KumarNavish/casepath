\section{Confirmatory test}
\label{sec:test}

We tested \casepath at its frozen commit against every single-agent strategy of Section~\ref{sec:related} and against a frontier model, on sealed data and under a plan fixed in advance. H1 to H3 were confirmed, H4 was not, and H5 was therefore not tested.

\subsection{Data and freeze}
\label{sec:test-data}

The test data, \texttt{fresh2/}, were built and sealed before any design ran on them (Appendix~\ref{app:datasets}). Theft has \sthreeStories{} new stories (\sthreeStoryFirst--\sthreeStoryLast), each a different kind of case document with the condition sentences embedded in running text: \sthreePairsChanged{} changed-fact pairs and \sthreePairsUnresolved{} unresolved-fact pairs from \sthreeTexts{} texts. Tenancy has \sthreeClaims{} new claims in \sthreeFamilies{} families, \sthreeClaimsEnglish{} in English and \sthreeClaimsGerman{} in Swiss Standard German, with \sthreeAttachments{} attachments, \sthreeAttachmentsSufficient{} of them sufficient. The plan defines three subsets from the reference: \sthreeClaimsDenied{} \emph{denied-event} claims, whose dispute's defining event is false and stated so; \sthreeClaimsOpenEvent{} \emph{open-event} claims, which leave the defining event open; and \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} \emph{ordinary} claims, of which \sthreeClaimsOpenBranch{} raise one other fact and leave it open.

Development used the Study~1 data, \texttt{fresh/} and \sthreeProbeClaims{} probe claims. Theft stories \sthreeDevStoryFirst--\sthreeDevStoryLast{} of \texttt{fresh/} were used to develop the product, and stories \sthreeValStoryFirst--\sthreeValStoryLast{} once for validation. The probe was written because \texttt{fresh/} has no claim whose defining event is denied or open and \texttt{fresh2/} has; it was written with knowledge of the test set's design but not of its texts. Development results are in the work log and are not evidence.

The system under test is the product package at commit \texttt{6a72159c}, the product branch's tip with the fix commits on top; \texttt{python -m casepath parity} gave the same source-tree hash for every product run as for the frozen tree (Section~\ref{sec:parity}). The plan was committed before any call on the data, and the data loader refused to start otherwise. All settings are the commit's defaults: \sthreeReadings{} readings that must agree, and no presumption for a fact any reading finds raised and left open. The two models are gpt-6-luna, the cost-efficient model, and gpt-6.1-terra, the frontier model.

The measures are those of Study~2, computed by the same functions (Appendix~\ref{app:study2}). In tenancy, \emph{strict unsafe errors per claim} count unnecessary requests, missed and handed-over required documents, premature requests and silent skips. \emph{All deviations per claim} add both kinds of wrong hold, so that a design cannot look good by holding. In theft, the measure is \emph{wrong + missed changes per pair} between the two texts of a changed-fact pair; unresolved-fact pairs count premature requests and pairs left otherwise unchanged.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\label{sec:test-hypotheses}

The plan states \sthreeHypotheses{} hypotheses in a fixed order: H1 theft and H2 tenancy against Rules in context on the same model; H3 tenancy against Rules in context on the frontier model; H4 theft against each of Self-consistency, Model executes rules and ReAct, all three required; H5 theft non-inferiority to Rules in context on the frontier model, margin \sthreeHFiveMargin. Each is tested at the \sthreeAlpha{} level only if all before it were confirmed. The tests are exact two-sided sign-flip tests over clusters, the \sthreeTheftClusters{} changed facts and the \sthreeTenancyClusters{} families, and a hypothesis of fewer errors also needs the difference to favour \casepath. With \sthreeTheftClusters{} clusters the smallest attainable $p$ is \sthreeMinPTheft.

\paragraph{H1, confirmed}
On theft, with the same model and the same rules, \casepath made \sthreeHOneA{} wrong + missed changes per pair against \sthreeHOneB{} for Rules in context (difference \sthreeHOneDiff, interval \sthreeHOneLo{} to \sthreeHOneHi, $p\sthreeHOnePeq$).

\paragraph{H2, confirmed}
On tenancy, with the same model, \casepath made \sthreeHTwoA{} strict unsafe errors per claim against \sthreeHTwoB{} ($p\sthreeHTwoPeq$).

\paragraph{H3, confirmed}
On tenancy, against the frontier model, \casepath made \sthreeHThreeA{} strict unsafe errors per claim against \sthreeHThreeB{} ($p\sthreeHThreePeq$).

\paragraph{H4, not confirmed}
H4 required \casepath to make fewer theft errors than each of the three prior-work strategies. It made fewer than Model executes rules (\sthreeHFourMERA{} against \sthreeHFourMERB, $p\sthreeHFourMERPeq$) and ReAct (\sthreeHFourReactA{} against \sthreeHFourReactB, $p\sthreeHFourReactPeq$). Against Self-consistency the values were \sthreeHFourSCA{} against \sthreeHFourSCB{} (interval \sthreeHFourSCLo{} to \sthreeHFourSCHi), and the test over \sthreeHFourSCClusters{} changed facts gave $p\sthreeHFourSCPeq$. \casepath had fewer errors on \sthreePostSCFavourCasePath{} changed facts and more on \sthreePostSCFavourSelfCons{} (Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}). The hypothesis failed.

\paragraph{H5, not tested}
Because H4 failed, H5 was not tested. Outside the procedure, \casepath made \sthreeHFiveA{} wrong + missed changes per pair against \sthreeHFiveB{} for the frontier model with the same rules, and \sthreeSecFourBA{} against \sthreeSecFourBB{} with the original rules.

\paragraph{Where the tenancy difference lies}
The plan predicted that H2 and H3 would be carried by the claims whose defining event is denied or open, and so they were. On the \sthreeClaimsDeniedOpen{} such claims, \casepath made \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenA{} strict unsafe errors per claim, against \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenB{} for Rules in context and \sthreeHThreeDeniedOpenB{} for the frontier model. On the \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} ordinary claims the values were \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryA{} against \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryB{} and \sthreeHThreeOrdinaryB{}. These values are means of family values, as in the tests; per-claim values are in Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}. Against the frontier model the ordinary claims show no difference ($p\sthreeHThreeOrdinaryPeq$), and non-inferiority at margin \sthreeSecOneAMargin{} holds as a secondary result ($p\sthreeSecOneAPeq$). Against the same model the ordinary claims also show a difference (\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryDiff, $p\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryPeq$, descriptive), which the plan did not predict.

\paragraph{Open facts}
\casepath held what the file left open (Table~\ref{tab:s3-open}). On theft it left \sthreeCasePathThOpenUnchanged\% of unresolved-fact pairs otherwise unchanged, against \sthreeRicThOpenUnchanged--\sthreeReactThOpenUnchanged\% for every single-prompt design on either model with the reviewed rules; the frontier single prompt with the original rules left \sthreeRicOrigFrontierThOpenUnchanged\% unchanged. On tenancy it held \sthreeCasePathTenCorrectHold\% of the documents the reference holds, against \sthreeRicTenCorrectHold\% for Rules in context and \sthreeRicFrontierTenCorrectHold\% for the frontier model. Two-valued execution of the product's own readings fails on open facts by construction. With unresolved taken as false it skipped \sthreeTwoValFalseOpenEventSilentSkip{} held documents per open-event claim; with unresolved taken as true it requested \sthreeTwoValTrueOpenEventPremature{} of them too early. On theft, where only requests are scored, the first variant gives \casepath's own requests.

\paragraph{All deviations and the cost of holding}
Counting wrong holds as errors, \casepath made \sthreeCasePathTenAllDev{} deviations per claim, against \sthreeRicTenAllDev{} for Rules in context ($p\sthreeSecThreeAPeq$) and \sthreeRicFrontierTenAllDev{} for the frontier model ($p\sthreeSecThreeBPeq$). \casepath holds more than the single prompts: \sthreeCasePathTenSpurHold{} holds per claim of documents the reference neither requires nor holds, against \sthreeRicTenSpurHold{} and \sthreeRicFrontierTenSpurHold. Most of them fall on denied-event claims (\sthreeCasePathDeniedSpurHold{} per claim), where the readings did not all agree on the denial. It also held \sthreeCasePathTenHeldReq{} required documents per claim, against \sthreeRicTenHeldReq{} and \sthreeRicFrontierTenHeldReq.

\paragraph{Which agents matter, and what they cost}
The checklist-only setting did as well as the full product on every measure: \sthreeChecklistTenStrict{} against \sthreeCasePathTenStrict{} strict unsafe errors per claim ($p\sthreeSecFiveAPeq$) and \sthreeChecklistThErrors{} against \sthreeCasePathThErrors{} wrong + missed changes per pair ($p\sthreeSecFiveCPeq$). The two runs are not fully independent: \sthreeSharedTenCallShare\% and \sthreeSharedThCallShare\% of the full product's calls returned responses first paid by the checklist-only run (Appendix~\ref{app:dev-study3}). Against the Core outside the product, \casepath made \sthreeCasePathThErrors{} against \sthreeCoreThErrors{} ($p\sthreeSecFiveDPeq$). In no case-run did a need added by a later agent enter the scored checklist (\sthreeChangesCasePathTenEntered{} of \sthreeChangesCasePathTenCaseRuns{} tenancy and \sthreeChangesCasePathThEntered{} of \sthreeChangesCasePathThCaseRuns{} theft case-runs); the agents added \sthreeChangesCasePathTenAdded{} and \sthreeChangesCasePathThAdded{} needs, all as suggestions. The checklist-only setting costs \sthreeCostChecklistShareTen\% and \sthreeCostChecklistShareTh\% of the full product (Figure~\ref{fig:agents}). The full product costs USD~\sthreeCasePathTenCost{} per tenancy claim and USD~\sthreeCasePathThCost{} per theft text; checklist only USD~\sthreeChecklistTenCost{} and USD~\sthreeChecklistThCost; the frontier single prompt USD~\sthreeRicFrontierTenCost{} and USD~\sthreeRicFrontierThCost. The full product therefore costs more than the frontier single prompt, \sthreeCasePathTenRatio{} times on tenancy and \sthreeCasePathThRatio{} times on theft. Checklist only costs \sthreeChecklistTenRatio{} and \sthreeChecklistThRatio{} times as much.

\paragraph{Model for model}
The plan stated no hypothesis for \casepath on the frontier model, which ran once per case. On tenancy it made \sthreeFrontierChecklistTenStrict{} strict unsafe errors per claim, against \sthreeCasePathTenStrict{} for \casepath on the cost-efficient model ($p\sthreeSecSevenDPeq$) and \sthreeRicFrontierTenStrict{} for the frontier single prompt. On theft it was not better than the frontier single prompt: \sthreeFrontierChecklistThErrors{} against \sthreeRicFrontierThErrors{} wrong + missed changes per pair ($p\sthreeSecSevenAPeq$). The gain from executing rules is largest for the cost-efficient model. On the frontier model the product cost USD~\sthreeFrontierChecklistTenCost{} per claim and USD~\sthreeFrontierChecklistThCost{} per text.

\paragraph{Robustness}
Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3} gives the secondary tables. In both languages \casepath made fewer strict unsafe errors per claim than every single-prompt design (English \sthreeCasePathEnglishStrict{} against at least \sthreeMerEnglishStrict, German \sthreeCasePathGermanStrict{} against at least \sthreeMerGermanStrict). \casepath's requests agreed more between runs than those of Rules in context (Jaccard \sthreeCasePathTenJaccard{} against \sthreeRicTenJaccard{} on tenancy, \sthreeCasePathThJaccard{} against \sthreeRicThJaccard{} on theft). Counting an error repeated across a story's pairs once, \casepath made \sthreeCasePathThErrorsDedup{} theft errors per pair against \sthreeRicThErrorsDedup{} for Rules in context and \sthreeSelfConsThErrorsDedup{} for Self-consistency. ReAct returned \sthreeRunFailedReact{} failed outputs, scored as empty plans as planned. A post-hoc analysis that leaves them out gives \sthreePostReactThA{} against \sthreePostReactThB{} on theft ($p\sthreePostReactThPeq$) and \sthreePostReactTenStrictA{} against \sthreePostReactTenStrictB{} on tenancy ($p\sthreePostReactTenStrictPeq$).

\paragraph{Irregularities}
Of \sthreeRunInvocations{} invocations of the run, \sthreeRunNotComplete{} stopped before the run was complete; each was resumed from the response cache with the analysis settings unchanged. \sthreeRunFailedOutputs{} outputs failed, none from the product and \sthreeRunFailedReact{} from ReAct. The run started before the product's owners confirmed the merge of the tested commit. Appendix~\ref{app:dev-study3} lists every irregularity and every departure from the plan's text.

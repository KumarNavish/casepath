\section{Evaluation (Study 3)}
\label{sec:test}

We tested \casepath at a frozen commit against designs that give a model the same files and the same rules, on sealed synthetic data under a plan fixed in advance. We call this test Study~3. It has two data sets, theft and tenancy, each with its own workflow and measures. Section~\ref{sec:test-hypotheses} gives the registered outcomes. Sections~\ref{sec:test-cells} to~\ref{sec:test-lint} give checks added after an outside review of the results; they are not registered, and none changes a hypothesis's status.

\subsection{Data, designs and freeze}
\label{sec:test-data}

The test data, \texttt{fresh2/}, were built and sealed before any design ran on them (Supplement, Section~\ref{S-app:datasets}). Theft has \sthreeStories{} new stories: \sthreePairsChanged{} \emph{changed-fact pairs}, two texts that differ in one sentence that changes one fact, and \sthreePairsUnresolved{} \emph{unresolved-fact pairs}, in which the second text leaves that fact open. Tenancy has \sthreeClaims{} new claims in \sthreeFamilies{} families, half in English and half in Swiss Standard German. Each tenancy dispute has a \emph{defining event}, such as receiving a written rent-increase notification. The plan defines three subsets from the reference: \sthreeClaimsDenied{} \emph{denied-event} claims, which say the event has not happened; \sthreeClaimsOpenEvent{} \emph{open-event} claims, which leave it open; and \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} \emph{ordinary} claims, of which \sthreeClaimsOpenBranch{} leave another fact open. The test set was designed to include the denied- and open-event claims.

Three limits apply to every result. The tenancy references were produced by the engine \casepath implements, under a silence clause that we wrote and that every design receives. One model wrote every text, and a model of the same family checked them blind. The texts have 77--320 words, so the product's paths for long files were never exercised.

An AI agent session wrote the texts from a brief that held only the data design. It could read the repository, including the product's prompts and development results, so knowledge of the product may have shaped the texts in ways no check here can detect. The session that directed development, drafted the plan and chose the open rule had read the test set's design and counts, not its texts. Development effort was one-sided: \casepath was revised over three studies on development data, while no comparator text changed after 19:10 on 10~November. The open rule (Section~\ref{sec:configuration}) was set after a probe on \sthreeProbeClaims{} development claims, before the freeze: an independent AI review session of the product's code found a defect, and the directing author chose the rule \texttt{any}, overruling a criterion on wrong holds the author had set earlier. Table~\ref{tab:s3-timeline} gives the dates. At the time of writing, the product's owners have not confirmed the merge of the tested commit.

The measures are Study~2's. On tenancy, \emph{strict unsafe errors per claim} count unnecessary requests, missed and handed-over required documents, premature requests and silent skips; \emph{all deviations per claim} add held documents that should have been requested and \emph{unneeded holds}, documents the reference neither requires nor holds. A design cannot look good by holding. On theft, \emph{errors per pair} count the document changes between the two texts of a changed-fact pair that are wrong or missing; unresolved-fact pairs count pairs left otherwise unchanged.

\subsection{Registered outcomes}
\label{sec:test-hypotheses}

Three of five hypotheses were confirmed, one failed and one was not tested (Table~\ref{tab:s3-hypotheses}). The plan tested them in a fixed order, each at the \sthreeAlpha{} level only if all before it were confirmed: H1 theft and H2 tenancy against Rules in context on the same model; H3 tenancy against Rules in context on the frontier model; H4 theft against each of Self-consistency, Model executes rules and ReAct, all three required; H5 theft non-inferiority to Rules in context on the frontier model. H1 to H4 use exact two-sided sign-flip tests over clusters, the \sthreeTheftClusters{} changed facts on theft and the \sthreeTenancyClusters{} families on tenancy.

As the plan predicted, most of the tenancy difference lies in the \sthreeClaimsDeniedOpen{} denied- and open-event claims: \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenA{} strict unsafe errors per claim against \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenB{} and \sthreeHThreeDeniedOpenB{} for the two single prompts. On the \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} ordinary claims, \casepath made \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryA{} against \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryB{} for the same model ($p\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryPeq$, which the plan did not predict) and \sthreeHThreeOrdinaryB{} for the frontier model ($p\sthreeHThreeOrdinaryPeq$, no difference). These subset rows are descriptive family means (Supplement, Table~\ref{S-tab:s3-subsets}).

H4 required fewer theft errors than each of three prior-work strategies and failed against Self-consistency. The registered test gives each of the nine cluster differences a random sign; its smallest attainable $p$ is \sthreeFThreeSCMinP, and it returned \sthreeFThreeSCP, although \casepath had the lower mean in all nine clusters. The differences are unequal in size, so sums as far from zero are not rare. An interval from inverting this test, added after review, runs from \sthreeRFiveHFourSCInvertedLo{} to \sthreeRFiveHFourSCInvertedHi{} and includes zero. With the eight stories as clusters instead, $p=\sthreeRFiveHFourSCStoriesP$; this changes no status. H5 was therefore not tested.

We changed the order of the hypotheses 38 minutes before the freeze, on development evidence. In the plan's final draft (21:26) the tenancy contrast against the frontier model came fifth. At 21:51 we moved it to third place, with the recorded reason that the contrasts development supported most strongly should come first. No design or scorer had then touched the sealed data. Under the order of 21:26, H3 would not have been tested. Under Holm's correction over the five hypotheses, which we did not register, H1, H2, H3 and H5 would pass and H4 would not. The registered outcome stands: three confirmed, one failed, one not tested.

The decomposition, added after review, attributes each error to the stage that caused it. The run's stored state is replaced stage by stage, in a fixed order, by its designed value; the error that disappears at a stage is that stage's part, and what remains with every stage right is the rule's. The parts add up exactly, and their sum does not depend on the order. On tenancy the rule part is zero by construction, because the reference comes from the engine; most of the cost-efficient product's errors are readings that admitted a wrong value, mainly a denied defining event read as stated. On theft, the rules alone cause \spostDecThLunaRule{} errors per pair in both cells. The single prompts have no observable stage. Their tenancy errors are concentrated by kind: on denied-event claims, \spostKindTenRicFrontierDeniedUnnecessary{} to \spostKindTenSelfConsDeniedUnnecessary{} unnecessary requests per claim for Rules in context on either model and Self-consistency; on open-event claims, \spostKindTenSelfConsOpenEventPremature{} to \spostKindTenRicFrontierOpenEventPremature{} premature requests. Supplement, Section~\ref{S-supp:checks} gives the tables by subset.

The agents outside the core made no detectable difference in errors. \casepath, checklist only, made \sthreeChecklistTenStrict{} against \sthreeCasePathTenStrict{} strict unsafe errors per claim ($p\sthreeSecFiveAPeq$) and \sthreeChecklistThErrors{} against \sthreeCasePathThErrors{} errors per pair ($p\sthreeSecFiveCPeq$), at \sthreeCostChecklistShareTen\% and \sthreeCostChecklistShareTh\% of the full product's cost. The two settings shared cached responses, so this is no independent repetition.

\subsection{Does the result depend on our reading of the rules?}
\label{sec:test-readings}

Two blind evaluators reproduced the tenancy references exactly. Under a rejected reading of the rule text, the product's unchanged workflow can lose its lead; once the workflow encodes the reading, the lead holds under every reading we can read as evidence (Table~\ref{tab:readings}; added after review, not registered). The tenancy references come from the engine \casepath implements, so a reviewer may object that the test rewards the product's own reading of the rules. We answered in three steps.

First, a clean room. Two AI evaluator sessions, which never saw the engine, its outputs or the reference, each wrote an evaluator from the rule text and the designed fact states alone. Their references agreed with the registered one on \sthreeFFiveEvalASame{} of \sthreeFFiveEvalADocuments{} and \sthreeFFiveEvalBSame{} of \sthreeFFiveEvalBDocuments{} document decisions. No person checked their work. After delivering its hashed reference, one session listed a scratch directory and saw the names of other files of this work; by its report it opened none.

Second, the product's unchanged workflow scored against references written under four readings that the evaluators had rejected. Under A1, ``a denied defining event does not stop the process'', the tenancy result reverses: the difference from Rules in context becomes $+\sthreeFFiveAOneHTwoDiff$ ($p=\sthreeFFiveAOneHTwoP$) and from the frontier prompt $+\sthreeFFiveAOneHThreeDiff$ ($p=\sthreeFFiveAOneHThreeP$), and two all-deviation contrasts reverse significantly. With all four readings together, H2's contrast reverses significantly ($+\sthreeFFiveAAllHTwoDiff$, $p=\sthreeFFiveAAllHTwoP$). Under A2, A3 and A4 the result holds. This step measures the workflow: a workflow written under the wrong reading is wrong in every file the reading affects.

Third, the workflow amended to each reading, with the checklist recomputed from the stored readings and no model calls. The unamended replay reproduces \sthreeFFiveBReproducesCasePath{} of \sthreeFFiveBReplayedCasePath{} stored checklists. \casepath then keeps a significant lead over both single prompts under every reading. It is smaller under A1 and A2. Under A1 the lead consists of the open-event claims; under A2, of the denied-event claims. The baselines were not re-run, and their prompts carried the registered rule text. Under A1 this favours them, because a prompt that ignored the registered stop is scored as right. Under A3 and ``all four'' it works against them, because they were never told the reading. Those two rows are upper bounds; only A1, A2 and A4 are read as evidence.

\subsection{Rules as the limit, and a lint that finds them}
\label{sec:test-lint}

On theft with the frontier model the limit is the rules, and the lint found the faulty conditions but its remedy did not survive out of sample (Table~\ref{tab:lint}; added after review, not registered). The decomposition splits that cell's \spostDecThTerraMeasured{} errors per pair into \spostDecThTerraRule{} from the rules, \spostDecThTerraReading{} from readings and \spostDecThTerraHold{} from holds. With every fact read correctly, the reviewed rules still give \spostDecThTerraRule. Most of the rest comes from two over-broad conditions read literally. One requires a third party at fault: the frontier model admitted it in every ordinary text, on a sentence excluding any third party ``apart from the unidentified thief''. The other concerns traces of the theft that were altered, whose value changed between the two texts of a pair.

The lint flagged these conditions without reference answers. A mutation test checked it on tenancy, with one model and one run per claim, for USD~\spostMutSpendUSD. We widened \spostMutPlanted{} conditions to general circumstances, and the lint flagged \spostMutPlantedFlagged. \spostMutPlantedAlready{} of them were flagged already in their original wording, so the informative count is \spostMutPlantedNew{} of \spostMutPlantedNew. \spostMutFalseAlarmMutated{} of \spostMutUnmutated{} untouched conditions were flagged in both arms. On the Study~3 tenancy claims the lint flags \spostTenLintLunaFlagged{} of \spostTenLintLunaConditions{} conditions with the cost-efficient model's readings and \spostTenLintTerraFlagged{} of \spostTenLintTerraConditions{} with the frontier model's, without any mutation. Those claims were built with several true branch facts each and are not ordinary cases. The lint therefore detects blunt over-breadth; its false-alarm rate is unknown and needs files in which a condition is known not to apply.

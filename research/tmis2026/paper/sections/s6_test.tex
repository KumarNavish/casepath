\section{Confirmatory test (Study 3)}
\label{sec:test}

We tested \casepath at a frozen commit against every single-agent strategy of Section~\ref{sec:related} and against a frontier model, on sealed data and under a plan fixed in advance. We call this test Study~3. H1 to H3 were confirmed, H4 was not, and H5 was therefore not tested.

\subsection{Data and freeze}
\label{sec:test-data}

The test data, \texttt{fresh2/}, were built and sealed before any design ran on them (Appendix~\ref{app:datasets}). Theft has \sthreeStories{} new stories (\sthreeStoryFirst--\sthreeStoryLast), each a different kind of case document: \sthreePairsChanged{} changed-fact pairs and \sthreePairsUnresolved{} unresolved-fact pairs from \sthreeTexts{} texts. Tenancy has \sthreeClaims{} new claims in \sthreeFamilies{} families, \sthreeClaimsEnglish{} in English and \sthreeClaimsGerman{} in Swiss Standard German, with \sthreeAttachments{} attachments, \sthreeAttachmentsSufficient{} of them sufficient. Each tenancy dispute has a defining event, such as receiving a written rent-increase notification. The plan defines three subsets from the reference: \sthreeClaimsDenied{} \emph{denied-event} claims, which say the defining event has not happened; \sthreeClaimsOpenEvent{} \emph{open-event} claims, which leave it open; and \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} \emph{ordinary} claims, of which \sthreeClaimsOpenBranch{} raise one other fact and leave it open. The test set was designed to include the denied- and open-event claims.

Three limits apply to every result below. The tenancy references are produced by the engine \casepath implements, under a silence clause that we wrote and that every design receives. One model wrote every text, and a model of the same family checked them blind. The texts have 77--320 words, so \casepath's paths for long files (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) were never exercised.

The texts of \texttt{fresh2/} were written by an AI agent session working in the repository, from a brief that contained only the data design. That session had read access to the repository, including the product's prompts and development results, and was instructed to consult no design output; it implemented no product change. The blind check ran in separate sessions of another model of the same family. Data writing and product development were directed by the same author through separate agent sessions. The risk is that knowledge of the product's prompts or failures shaped the texts in ways no check here can detect.

Development used the Study~1 data, \texttt{fresh/} and \sthreeProbeClaims{} probe claims. Theft stories \sthreeDevStoryFirst--\sthreeDevStoryLast{} of \texttt{fresh/} were used to develop the product; stories \sthreeValStoryFirst--\sthreeValStoryLast{} were used once for validation and re-run from cached readings after the last fixes. The probe, claims whose defining event is denied or open, was written with knowledge of the test set's design but not of its texts. Development effort was one-sided: \casepath was revised over three studies on development data, while the comparators' texts were written for Studies~1 and~2 or adapted from them, and were last changed before the probe (Appendix~\ref{app:baseline-prompts}). Development results are in the work log and are not evidence.

The open rule (Section~\ref{sec:configuration}) was set after the probe. An independent AI review session of the product's code found that a single ``not stated'' let a presumption override a doubt two readings had quoted. The directing author then chose the rule \texttt{any}, overruling a numerical criterion on wrong holds that the author had set earlier; this was before the freeze and before any design read a sealed datum. The system under test is the product at commit \texttt{6a72159c}; the parity command gave its source-tree hash for every product run (Section~\ref{sec:parity}). The plan was committed before any call on the data, and the data loader refused to start otherwise. Table~\ref{tab:s3-timeline} gives the dates.

All designs reached the models through OpenRouter's chat-completions API: gpt-6-luna, the cost-efficient model, and gpt-6.1-terra, the frontier model. The responses name them with the prefix \texttt{openai/}, served by OpenAI; no snapshot or version date is recorded (Appendix~\ref{app:dev-study3}). The harness asked for low reasoning effort in every comparator call. The product's settings are the commit's defaults plus the reasoning flag turned on, as the plan specifies: \sthreeReadings{} readings, the open rule \texttt{any} and full run scope, with low or medium reasoning effort per call (Section~\ref{sec:configuration}). No design sent a temperature, top-p or seed, so the provider's defaults applied; they are not recorded. The harness keys every response by the request's address, its full body and the run index. The readings of one run send the same instructions and files under different response-schema names, so they are three calls; the same reading in another run differs in the run index.

The measures are Study~2's, computed by the same functions (Supplement, Section~\ref{S-app:study2}). In tenancy, \emph{strict unsafe errors per claim} count unnecessary requests, missed and handed-over required documents, premature requests and silent skips. \emph{All deviations per claim} add both kinds of wrong hold, so that a design cannot look good by holding. In theft, \emph{wrong + missed changes per pair} count the document changes between the two texts of a changed-fact pair that are wrong or missing; unresolved-fact pairs count premature requests and pairs left otherwise unchanged.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\label{sec:test-hypotheses}

The plan states \sthreeHypotheses{} hypotheses in a fixed order: H1 theft and H2 tenancy against Rules in context on the same model; H3 tenancy against Rules in context on the frontier model; H4 theft against each of Self-consistency, Model executes rules and ReAct, all three required; H5 theft non-inferiority to Rules in context on the frontier model, margin \sthreeHFiveMargin. Each is tested at the \sthreeAlpha{} level only if all before it were confirmed. H1 to H4 use exact two-sided sign-flip tests over clusters, the \sthreeTheftClusters{} changed facts and the \sthreeTenancyClusters{} families, and a hypothesis of fewer errors also needs the difference to favour \casepath; with \sthreeTheftClusters{} clusters the smallest attainable $p$ is \sthreeMinPTheft. H5 uses a paired cluster-bootstrap non-inferiority test.

\paragraph{H1, confirmed}
On theft, with the same model and the same rules, \casepath made \sthreeHOneA{} wrong + missed changes per pair against \sthreeHOneB{} for Rules in context ($p\sthreeHOnePeq$). The theft rules had been reviewed for execution, with knowledge of the test conditions (the review is in Section~\ref{sec:history}). Against Rules in context with the original rules the difference remained large on the same model (\sthreeSecFourCA{} against \sthreeSecFourCB, $p\sthreeSecFourCPeq$) and was small on the frontier model (\sthreeSecFourBA{} against \sthreeSecFourBB); both are secondary contrasts.

\paragraph{H2 and H3, confirmed}
On tenancy, \casepath made \sthreeHTwoA{} strict unsafe errors per claim against \sthreeHTwoB{} for the same model ($p\sthreeHTwoPeq$) and \sthreeHThreeB{} for the frontier model ($p\sthreeHThreePeq$).

\paragraph{H4, not confirmed}
H4 required fewer theft errors than each of three prior-work strategies. \casepath made fewer than Model executes rules (\sthreeHFourMERA{} against \sthreeHFourMERB, $p\sthreeHFourMERPeq$) and ReAct (\sthreeHFourReactA{} against \sthreeHFourReactB, $p\sthreeHFourReactPeq$). Against Self-consistency the values were \sthreeHFourSCA{} against \sthreeHFourSCB, and the test over \sthreeHFourSCClusters{} changed facts gave $p\sthreeHFourSCPeq$; \casepath had fewer errors on \sthreePostSCFavourCasePath{} changed facts and more on \sthreePostSCFavourSelfCons{} (post hoc; Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}). The hypothesis failed. The registered percentile bootstrap interval (\sthreeRFiveHFourSCBootLo{} to \sthreeRFiveHFourSCBootHi) excludes zero because it resamples nine differences, \sthreePostSCFavourCasePath{} favouring \casepath, and with so few clusters it is too narrow. The exact test, the registered one, asks how often random signs on these unequal differences give a sum as large, and its inverted interval includes zero. With the eight stories as clusters instead, a sensitivity added after review, H4's Self-consistency contrast gave $p=\sthreeRFiveHFourSCStoriesP$; it changes no status (Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}).

\paragraph{H5, not tested}
Because H4 failed, H5 was not tested. \casepath made \sthreeHFiveA{} wrong + missed changes per pair against \sthreeHFiveB{} for the frontier model with the same rules (Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}).

\paragraph{Where the tenancy difference lies}
The plan predicted that H2 and H3 would be carried by the denied- and open-event claims, which the test set was designed to include, and most of the difference lies there. On these \sthreeClaimsDeniedOpen{} claims \casepath made \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenA{} strict unsafe errors per claim, against \sthreeHTwoDeniedOpenB{} for Rules in context and \sthreeHThreeDeniedOpenB{} for the frontier model. On the \sthreeClaimsOrdinary{} ordinary claims the values were \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryA{} against \sthreeHTwoOrdinaryB{} and \sthreeHThreeOrdinaryB{} (family means, descriptive). Against the frontier model the ordinary claims did not differ significantly ($p\sthreeHThreeOrdinaryPeq$). Against the same model they also differed (\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryDiff, $p\sthreeHTwoOrdinaryPeq$, descriptive), which the plan did not predict.

\paragraph{Exact deferral against two-valued and strong Kleene evaluation (added after review)}
On theft, treating unresolved facts as false gives exactly \casepath's requests in \sthreeRElevenThIdentical{} of \sthreeRElevenThTextRuns{} text-runs, because the theft measures score requests only; exact deferral adds nothing there that they can see. On tenancy, \sthreeRElevenTenWithOpen{} of \sthreeRElevenTenEvaluated{} evaluated claim-runs left a workflow fact without a value. Exact deferral changed the checklist against ``unresolved = false'' in \sthreeRElevenTenDiffFalse{} of them, and against strong Kleene evaluation of the same values in \sthreeRElevenTenDiffKleene.

\paragraph{All deviations and the cost of holding}
Counting wrong holds as errors, \casepath made \sthreeCasePathTenAllDev{} deviations per claim, against \sthreeRicTenAllDev{} for Rules in context ($p\sthreeSecThreeAPeq$) and \sthreeRicFrontierTenAllDev{} for the frontier model ($p\sthreeSecThreeBPeq$). \casepath holds more than the single prompts: \sthreeCasePathTenSpurHold{} holds per claim of documents the reference neither requires nor holds, against \sthreeRicTenSpurHold{} and \sthreeRicFrontierTenSpurHold. Most fall on denied-event claims (\sthreeCasePathDeniedSpurHold{} per claim), where the defining event stayed unresolved.

Section~\ref{sec:fate} and Table~\ref{tab:fact-accuracy} give how often \casepath's admitted values and presumptions were wrong.

\paragraph{Stress test of presumptions (added after review)}
The texts leave every unmentioned branch fact false, so no presumption was wrong by design and wrong presumptions were not otherwise tested. We made each of the \sthreeRFourAbsentFacts{} unmentioned branch facts true with probability 5, 10 or 20\%, recomputed the engine's references and rescored every stored checklist (\sthreeRDraws{} draws each). \casepath's strict unsafe errors per claim rose from \sthreeHTwoA{} to \sthreeRFourFiveCasePath, \sthreeRFourTenCasePath{} and \sthreeRFourTwentyCasePath. The single prompts, which received the same silence clause, rose by about as much, so the difference from Rules in context stayed at \sthreeRFourTwentyDiffRic{} and from the frontier model at \sthreeRFourTwentyDiffRicFrontier{} even at 20\% (Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3}).

\paragraph{What the single prompts did on denied-event claims (added after review)}
The single prompts mostly saw the denial and still requested documents. On the \sthreeREightRicRuns{} denied-event claim-runs, Rules in context asked in \sthreeREightRicAck{} whether the notice had since arrived and requested documents in \sthreeREightRicRequests; on the frontier model it asked in \sthreeREightRicFrontierAck{} and requested in \sthreeREightRicFrontierRequests. A typical question beside the requests: ``Have you since received the official rent-increase notice? If so, when and how was it delivered?''

\paragraph{Why the reviewed rules hurt the single prompts (added after review)}
With the reviewed theft rules Rules in context made more errors than with the original ones: \sthreeRNineLunaErrReviewed{} against \sthreeRNineLunaErrOriginal{} per pair on the same model, \sthreeRNineTerraErrReviewed{} against \sthreeRNineTerraErrOriginal{} on the frontier model. The prompts had about the same length (median \sthreeRNineLunaTokensReviewed{} against \sthreeRNineLunaTokensOriginal{} prompt tokens). The rule changes do not explain most of it: documents named by a changed rule carry \sthreeRNineLunaShareChangedRules{} and \sthreeRNineTerraShareChangedRules{} of the change in error counts, and the largest shifts are on documents no changed rule names. The stored outputs give no further explanation.

\paragraph{Which agents matter}
The agents that cannot decide the checklist are the claim reader's brief, the process builder and the result checker. No need added by an agent entered a scored checklist (\sthreeChangesCasePathTenEntered{} of \sthreeChangesCasePathTenCaseRuns{} tenancy and \sthreeChangesCasePathThEntered{} of \sthreeChangesCasePathThCaseRuns{} theft case-runs). The checklist-only setting showed no detectable difference in errors from the full product: \sthreeChecklistTenStrict{} against \sthreeCasePathTenStrict{} strict unsafe errors per claim ($p\sthreeSecFiveAPeq$) and \sthreeChecklistThErrors{} against \sthreeCasePathThErrors{} wrong + missed changes per pair ($p\sthreeSecFiveCPeq$). The two settings share cached responses (\sthreeSharedTenCallShare\% of the full product's calls on tenancy, \sthreeSharedThCallShare\% on theft), so this is no independent repetition. Their scored checklists were identical in \sthreeRSevenTenIdenticalShare{} of tenancy and \sthreeRSevenThIdenticalShare{} of theft case-runs (added after review); every theft difference begins at a fact value. The steps the checklist-only setting does not run (the three agents above, the retries after the check and, on theft, the document judgments) account for \sthreeRSevenTenNotCalledShare{} and \sthreeRSevenThNotCalledShare{} of a full run's cost; the setting costs \sthreeRSevenTenChecklistRatio{} and \sthreeRSevenThChecklistRatio{} of a full run. Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3} reconciles this with the registered split by agent.

\paragraph{Cost and time}
The full product costs USD~\sthreeCasePathTenCost{} per tenancy claim and USD~\sthreeCasePathThCost{} per theft text, checklist only USD~\sthreeChecklistTenCost{} and USD~\sthreeChecklistThCost, the frontier single prompt USD~\sthreeRicFrontierTenCost{} and USD~\sthreeRicFrontierThCost{} (Figure~\ref{fig:cost}). The full product thus costs \sthreeCasePathTenRatio{} times the frontier single prompt on tenancy and \sthreeCasePathThRatio{} times on theft. A full \casepath case-run took a median of \sthreeRTenCasePathTenMedian{}~s on tenancy and \sthreeRTenCasePathThMedian{}~s on theft (90th percentiles \sthreeRTenCasePathTenNinety{} and \sthreeRTenCasePathThNinety~s), checklist only \sthreeRTenChecklistTenMedian{} and \sthreeRTenChecklistThMedian~s; Rules in context's one call took \sthreeRTenRicTenMedian{} and \sthreeRTenRicThMedian~s (added after review). A product time is the wall-clock time of a case-run among \sthreeRunWorkersLater{} in parallel, partly answered from the cache; a single-prompt time is one call's latency.

\paragraph{Robustness}
Appendix~\ref{app:rob-study3} gives the secondary tables. In both languages \casepath made fewer strict unsafe errors per claim than every single-prompt design, and its requests agreed more between runs than those of Rules in context. Counting an error repeated across a story's pairs once, \casepath made \sthreeCasePathThErrorsDedup{} theft errors per pair against \sthreeRicThErrorsDedup{} for Rules in context and \sthreeSelfConsThErrorsDedup{} for Self-consistency. A post-hoc analysis that leaves out ReAct's failed outputs gives \sthreePostReactThA{} against \sthreePostReactThB{} on theft ($p\sthreePostReactThPeq$).

\paragraph{The run}
The run took \sthreeRunInvocations{} invocations of the driver, from \sthreeRunFirstDate{} \sthreeRunFirstStart{} to \sthreeRunLastDate{} \sthreeRunLastEnd{} UTC, and \sthreeRunNotComplete{} of them stopped early. Of these stops, \sthreeRunDeliberateStops{} were deliberate: the operator interrupted the first invocation to raise the number of parallel workers, and its log line, which gives a wrong reason and a spend of zero, is corrected by a dated note; the wrapper stopped the fourth as planned, before a time limit. The other \sthreeRunFailureStops{} were failures of the environment: the session's time limit on background commands, and a container restart. Each later invocation resumed from the response cache with the same commit, settings, models, data and analysis; the number of workers and the spending cap changed. \sthreeRunFailedOutputs{} outputs failed, none from the product and \sthreeRunFailedReact{} from ReAct, and were scored as empty checklists as planned. The run started before the product's owners confirmed the merge of the tested commit. Appendix~\ref{app:dev-study3} lists every irregularity and every departure from the plan's text.

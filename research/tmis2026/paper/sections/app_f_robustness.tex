\subsection{Work after the test}
\label{app:rob-after}
This subsection gives the details of Section~\ref{sec:test-after}. All of it is post hoc, on development data (Section~\ref{app:dev-after}); the sources are \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak{}theft\_\allowbreak{}diagnosis.md}, \texttt{rule\_\allowbreak{}lint.md} and \texttt{rule\_\allowbreak{}lint\_\allowbreak{}eval.md} beside it, and \texttt{runs/\allowbreak{}speed/}; the numbers are generated by \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak{}make\_\allowbreak{}numbers\_\allowbreak{}follow\allowbreak{}up.py}.

\paragraph{The floor of the theft rules}
With every fact at its designed value, the product's evaluator makes \fuFloor{} wrong + missed changes per pair on the \fuFloorPairs{} changed-fact pairs of all \fuFloorStories{} stories, the same in every story and with either rule base; it leaves \fuFloorUnresUnchanged\% of unresolved-fact pairs unchanged. Each base has \fuFloorFaults{} faults. In both, the rule on misused SIM cards demands a bank statement together with the report of the SIM-card theft, and its cited exclusion clause names no bank statement; and the claim-form section on the person who caused the loss is demanded on every claim, so it can never be added when a contractor is identified. The reviewed base adds the expert-procedure document when a written request counts as choosing that procedure; the original base demands receipt and valuation of a scheduled valuable together where the reference accepts either. Two mappings are uncertain: whether a written request for the expert procedure makes the expert-route conditions true, and whether the third-party condition, read literally, includes the unidentified thief. Over them the floor lies between \fuFloorLo{} and \fuFloorHi. Converted into a workflow, each base gives the same requests and holds as the research engine on every text under every mapping, and switching any of the \fuConvAlternatives{} alternative evidence routes never lowers the floor: the conversion adds no error.

\paragraph{The single prompts and the floor}
The floor's errors are open to any design that applies the rules as written. The frontier single prompt makes them in \fuFloorSingleOrigShare\% of the cases where the floor has one with the original rules and \fuFloorSingleRevShare\% with the reviewed rules; the cost-efficient single prompt in \fuFloorSingleLunaOrigShare\% and \fuFloorSingleLunaRevShare\%. Below the floor a single prompt can go only by departing from the rules, which the frontier prompt does on the expert procedure with the reviewed rules, under the primary mapping.

\paragraph{What the frontier model misread}
On the frontier model, the third-party condition was admitted true in all \fuGTerraOffTexts{} ordinary base texts, and every one of its \fuGTerraSingleOff{} readings there answered true, quoting ``No identifiable third party caused or enlarged the loss apart from the unidentified thief.'' With the cost-efficient model it was admitted in none (\fuGLunaOffAdmitted). The condition on crime-scene traces changed between the texts of a story. In \fuTerraUnresChanged{} unresolved-fact pairs the frontier product's requests changed, all through these two conditions. Setting both to their designed values in the stored readings would lower the frontier product's errors from \fuDecTerraTotal{} to \fuTerraFixTwo{} per pair and leave \fuTerraFixTwoUnres\% of unresolved-fact pairs unchanged; with the two conditions whose wording is not a case fact also set, to \fuTerraFixFour. These are upper bounds that change nothing else. Under the other expert mapping, the floor in Table~\ref{tab:after-decomp} is \fuDecTerraFloorAlt{} and the misread changed fact \fuDecLunaChangedAlt, \fuDecLunaChkChangedAlt{} and \fuDecTerraChangedAlt{} for the three rows. Showing each condition's cited passages beside it in the reading did not change the third-party reading on two base texts (\fuPassagesCalls{} calls).

\paragraph{The reading instruction}
The instruction asked the readings to take what every case of the type involves as given and to answer a condition true only when the files state more. Its pre-set rule required, on two stories, no third-party admission in the base texts and fewer errors than a control arm and than the frontier single prompt with either rule base. It made \fuCaseTypeStoryEight{} and \fuCaseTypeStoryNine{} errors per pair, against \fuCaseTypeOrigEight{} and \fuCaseTypeOrigNine{} for the single prompt with the original rules, \fuCaseTypeRevEight{} and \fuCaseTypeRevNine{} with the reviewed rules, and \fuCaseTypeStoredEight{} for the stored product on the first story. But no defining condition of a theft was admitted, so the police report was requested in \fuCaseTypePoliceRequested{} of \fuCaseTypeTextRuns{} text-runs and held in \fuCaseTypePoliceHeld{}, against requested in \fuCaseTypeStoredPoliceRequested{} of \fuCaseTypeStoredTextRuns{} before. On the cost-efficient model (\fuCaseTypeLunaRuns{} runs) theft errors rose from \fuCaseTypeLunaOff{} to \fuCaseTypeLunaOn{} per pair and unresolved-fact pairs left unchanged fell from \fuCaseTypeLunaUnresOff\% to \fuCaseTypeLunaUnresOn\%; on \fuCaseTypeTenClaims{} tenancy claims, stated facts left open rose from \fuCaseTypeTenOpenOff{} to \fuCaseTypeTenOpenOn{} per claim and held documents required now from none to \fuCaseTypeTenHeldReqOn.

\paragraph{The lint}
A condition is flagged when the readings of a model admit it true in at least half of the ordinary base-text runs, or some reading answers true in at least three quarters of them; no reference answer, score or designed value is read. The stored readings of the tested commit give \fuLintOffRunsLuna{} ordinary runs for the cost-efficient model and \fuLintOffRunsTerra{} for the frontier model. The lint flagged \fuLintFlaggedLuna{} and \fuLintFlaggedTerra{} conditions, \fuLintFlaggedAll{} together; the condition on crime-scene traces and the third-party condition (admitted in \fuLintGFortyTwoTerra{} of \fuLintOffRunsTerra) were flagged with the frontier model only. The four-eyes review made \fuLintCaseType{} conditions unconditional, as the case type itself, narrowed \fuLintNarrowed{} and kept \fuLintKept, and did not adopt the proposed narrowing of the third-party condition, which one editor rejected; one rule was split, so the base grew from \fuLintRulesBefore{} to \fuLintRulesAfter{} rules. The editors left questions for a jurist (\texttt{RECONCILIATION.md}); three of them decide the floor here: the scope of the condition on crime-scene traces, when the insurer's instruction on measures is due, and whether an unidentified thief is a third party.

\paragraph{The reviewed rules without model calls}
Converted, the reviewed rules agree with the research engine on \fuLintEngineAssignments{} random assignments. They remove \fuLintDocsLeave{} documents from every ordinary checklist: the insurer's instruction on measures, and the consents of the police and of the insurer to altering traces or damaged items. With designed values, the floor rises from \fuLintFloorBefore{} to \fuLintFloorAfter{} if a planned repair counts as an alteration under the narrowed trace condition, and stays at \fuLintFloorAfterLow{} if it does not. Carried over to the frontier product's stored readings, the reviewed rules would give \fuLintPredTerraNever{} or \fuLintPredTerraDesign{} errors per pair, depending on how the new conditions are read; since every reading request changes with the rules, even this carry-over is an assumption. We therefore ran no model test.

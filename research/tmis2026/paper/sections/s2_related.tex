\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\casepath combines rules executed by code, models that read text, and an explicit value for what is not known. Each paragraph below names a line of work and what this article does differently; Table~\ref{tab:related} compares the closest systems.

\paragraph{Design science}
Design-science research in information systems builds an artefact for a class of problems and evaluates it \cite{hevner2004design,peffers2007design}, and presents design principles as its knowledge contribution \cite{gregor2013positioning}. We follow this pattern; unlike action design research, which builds and evaluates inside the organisation \cite{sein2011action}, our evaluation uses synthetic claims outside the insurer.

\paragraph{Rules over text}
Law and administration have executed rules since the British Nationality Act was written as a logic program \cite{sergot1986british}; Catala \cite{merigoux2021catala}, the Decision Model and Notation standard (DMN) \cite{omg2024dmn} and rules as code in government \cite{mohun2020cracking} continue the line. They take typed facts and treat a fact not supplied as false \cite{reiter1978closed} or send the decision to a default branch; \casepath reads facts from text and keeps them unresolved unless a stated presumption applies. Neuro-symbolic systems have a model translate each problem into logic that a solver executes \cite{pan2023logic,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm,gao2023pal}, and legal systems compute an answer, such as a tax liability, from a complete representation of a case \cite{holzenberger2023connecting,jurayj2026tax,kant2024equitable}; \casepath executes one reviewed workflow and must act before the case is complete. The closest system, LDPC, available as a preprint \cite{erwin2025ldpc}, answers yes/no questions as true, false or maybe and evaluates a model-written formula in strong Kleene logic, asking about a maybe; Proposition~\ref{prop:incomplete} shows what that evaluation costs when a workflow's branches meet again.

\paragraph{Case management and process models}
Case management \cite{omg2016cmmn}, declarative process models \cite{pesic2006declarative} and artifact-centric process models \cite{hull2008artifact} let a case's data decide which tasks and documents apply; they assume the data are entered, whereas \casepath reads them from the file and acts while some are unknown. Claims handling is such a knowledge-intensive process \cite{diciccio2015knowledge,eling2018impact,eling2022impact}; process research asks how language models can act within explicit constraints and keep people in control \cite{dumas2023aiaugmented,vidgof2023large,kampik2025large,konig2025unstructured,li2025textual}; we study one recurring step, deciding which documents to request.

\paragraph{Agents that apply the rules}
Agents usually follow a policy placed in their prompt, as $\tau$-bench, FlowBench and procedure-following benchmarks evaluate \cite{yao2025taubench,xiao2024flowbench,li2026sopbench}; Chain of Logic has the model evaluate a rule's elements \cite{servantez2024chain}, ReAct interleaves reasoning with tool calls \cite{yao2022react}, and self-consistency takes the majority of several runs \cite{wang2023selfconsistency}. In these designs the model applies the rules, and repeated runs differ \cite{atil2025nondeterminism,blairstanek2025unstable}. Section~\ref{sec:test} tests \casepath against a single prompt with the rules, self-consistency, a prompt that states the rules' semantics, and ReAct; it does not run Chain of Logic or the benchmarks' policy-in-prompt agents.

\paragraph{Reasoning over partial information}
Three-valued model checking gives an answer that holds for every completion of a partial state space \cite{bruns1999model}, partial models in software engineering reason over all concretisations of an uncertain model \cite{famelis2012partial}, and supervaluation \cite{vanfraassen1966singular} and certain answers over incomplete databases \cite{imielinski1984incomplete} do the same for sentences and queries. Exact deferral applies this idea to the documents a workflow needs; what is new is the application and the reconvergence case of Proposition~\ref{prop:incomplete}, not the semantics.

\paragraph{Presumptions}
Default logic draws conclusions that hold unless contradicted \cite{reiter1980logic}, and the Carneades model allocates the burden of proof in argument \cite{gordon2007carneades}. \casepath's presumptions are defaults of this kind, fixed per fact in the workflow with a source clause; the product does not reason about the burden of proof.

\paragraph{Asking, agreement and quotation}
Selective prediction abstains when a model's confidence is low \cite{chow1970optimum,geifman2017selective,kamath2020selective,wen2025abstention}, and interactive agents ask for missing information before they act \cite{li2024mediq,zhang2024askbeforeplan}. \casepath asks only when an open fact can change a document, a deterministic special case of the value of information \cite{howard1966information}. Semantic entropy measures uncertainty from the agreement of sampled answers \cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024detecting}, and citation benchmarks score whether cited sources support a model's statements \cite{gao2023enabling}; \casepath makes agreement and a found quotation hard conditions, checked by code. The nearest agent design, a preprint that is not peer reviewed, keeps a ledger of evidence obligations in code, tied to their sources \cite{kim2026evidenceobligation}.

\paragraph{Legal benchmarks and supervision}
LegalBench measures legal reasoning on 162 tasks \cite{guha2023legalbench}; our test measures how a design applies fixed rules to synthetic files, not legal reasoning. The EU AI Act classes AI systems for risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance as high-risk \cite{eu2024aiact}; evidence requests in legal-protection claims are not in that class, and we claim no regulatory standing for \casepath's records.

\paragraph{Reliance on advice}
Experts follow wrong advice \cite{jussupow2021augmenting,fugener2021humans}, automation bias affects novices and experts alike \cite{parasuraman2010complacency}, people turn away from algorithms after seeing them err \cite{dietvorst2015algorithm}, and hybrid intelligence asks how people and models complement each other \cite{dellermann2019hybrid,schemmer2023appropriate}. This article measures no reliance; the protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} is designed to.

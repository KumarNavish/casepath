\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\casepath combines three established ideas: rules executed by code, models that read text, and an explicit value for what is not known. No prior system we know combines all three for requesting evidence. Table~\ref{tab:related} compares the closest systems.

\paragraph{Rules over text}
Law and administration have executed rules since the British Nationality Act was written as a logic program \cite{sergot1986british}; Catala \cite{merigoux2021catala}, the Decision Model and Notation standard (DMN) \cite{omg2024dmn} and rules as code in government \cite{mohun2020cracking} continue the line. They take typed facts, not text. A fact that is not supplied is false, as under the closed-world assumption \cite{reiter1978closed}, or, in DMN, sends the decision to its default branch. Neuro-symbolic systems let a model translate each problem into logic or a program that a solver or interpreter executes \cite{pan2023logic,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm,gao2023pal}. The logic is written anew for each problem, and the solver takes every fact as given or false. Legal systems translate a statute once and compute an answer, such as a tax liability, from a complete representation of the case \cite{holzenberger2023connecting,jurayj2026tax,kant2024equitable}.

The closest system is LDPC \cite{erwin2025ldpc}. It splits a policy into yes/no questions, answers each with a natural-language-inference classifier as true, false or maybe, and has a model write a Boolean formula over the answers. Code evaluates the formula in strong Kleene logic, and a ``maybe'' yields a follow-up question. LDPC was evaluated on ShARC \cite{saeidi2018sharc}, where a system reads a short rule text and a user scenario and answers yes, no or irrelevant, or asks a follow-up question. Proposition~\ref{prop:incomplete} shows what strong Kleene evaluation costs when a workflow's branches meet again.

\paragraph{Agents that apply the rules}
Agents usually follow a policy placed in their prompt, the design that $\tau$-bench, FlowBench and procedure-following benchmarks evaluate \cite{yao2025taubench,xiao2024flowbench,li2026sopbench}. Chain of Logic has the model evaluate a rule's elements and recombine them \cite{servantez2024chain}. ReAct interleaves reasoning with tool calls \cite{yao2022react}, and self-consistency takes the majority answer of several runs \cite{wang2023selfconsistency}. In these designs the model applies the rules, and repeated runs give different answers \cite{atil2025nondeterminism,blairstanek2025unstable}. They are the single-agent strategies against which Section~\ref{sec:test} tests \casepath.

\paragraph{Abstention and three values}
Selective prediction lets a model abstain when its confidence is low \cite{chow1970optimum,geifman2017selective,kamath2020selective,wen2025abstention}, and interactive agents ask for missing information before they act \cite{li2024mediq,zhang2024askbeforeplan}. They decide from the model's confidence, not from whether an open fact can change a rule's outcome. Supervaluation \cite{vanfraassen1966singular} and certain answers over incomplete databases \cite{imielinski1984incomplete} keep only what holds under every completion. Exact deferral applies this idea to a workflow. The nearest agent design keeps a ledger of evidence obligations in code, planned by the model for each query, held open until resolved and tied to their sources \cite{kim2026evidenceobligation}.

\paragraph{Claims handling}
Claims handling is a knowledge-intensive process: the path a case takes depends on facts that emerge from documents and correspondence \cite{diciccio2015knowledge}. It is expected to be among the most automated parts of insurance \cite{eling2018impact,eling2022impact}. Process mining draws increasingly on unstructured data \cite{konig2025unstructured}, and language models analyse the text of insurance claims \cite{li2025textual}. Process research asks how such models can act within explicit constraints and keep people in control \cite{dumas2023aiaugmented,vidgof2023large,kampik2025large}. Deciding which documents to request is one recurring step; an error either delays the claim or lets it proceed without the evidence a decision needs.

\paragraph{Reliance on advice}
Experts follow wrong advice. Physicians given incorrect advice by a diagnostic system made more inaccurate diagnoses than without advice \cite{jussupow2021augmenting}. Advice from a model made people's answers converge and reduced what they knew that the model did not \cite{fugener2021humans}. Appropriate reliance has been defined as the pair of relative self-reliance and relative reliance on the advice \cite{schemmer2023appropriate}. Section~\ref{sec:experts} measures reliance on document requests in claims experts' own work.

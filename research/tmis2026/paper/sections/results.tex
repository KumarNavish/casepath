\subsection{Accuracy: hypotheses H1, H3 and H4}
\label{sec:results-accuracy}

\paragraph{Tenancy}
With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \casepath with exact deferral made \ctTerraHOneA{} unsafe errors per claim and \design{Rules in context} \ctTerraHOneB: \tenTerraCPxFP{} against \tenTerraBoneFP{} unnecessary requests and \tenTerraCPxMissN{} against \tenTerraBoneMissN{} missed documents over the three runs of 90 claims (Table~\ref{tab:tenancy}). The designs differed in only \ctTerraHOneNonzero{} of \ctTerraHOneClusters{} scenarios, so the test could not reach $p<\ptTerraHOneRaw$; the interval of the difference lies at or above zero. Counting handed-over documents as missed, \casepath was worse (\ctTerraHOneStrictA{} against \ctTerraHOneStrictB, $p=\ctTerraHOneStrictP$). With \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, the direction reversed: \ctLunaHOneA{} against \ctLunaHOneB{} unsafe errors per claim (\tenLunaCPxFP{} and \tenLunaCPxMissN{} against \tenLunaBoneFP{} and \tenLunaBoneMissN), $p=\ptLunaHOneRaw$ at its floor, \ptLunaHOne{} after Holm within the secondary analysis and \ptLunaHOneHolmAll{} across all ten tests. The strict measure reverses the direction again (\ctLunaHOneStrictA{} against \ctLunaHOneStrictB, $p=\ctLunaHOneStrictP$), because \casepath handed \tenLunaCPxHandedN{} required documents to a person, and majority voting over three runs of \design{Rules in context} narrowed the headline gap (\ctLunaHOneSCB{} unsafe errors per claim; $p=\ctLunaHOneSCP$ against \casepath).

\paragraph{Changed facts}
With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \design{Rules in context} made \thTerraRWrong{} wrong and \thTerraRMissed{} missed changes over the 81 pair runs ($F_1$ \thTerraRFone), \casepath \thTerraCPWrong{} and \thTerraCPMissed{} (\thTerraCPFone), and \design{Direct} \thTerraDWrong{} and \thTerraDMissed{} (\thTerraDFone), with its one failed output scored as an empty plan (Table~\ref{tab:theft}); H3 was not supported (difference \ctTerraHThreeRDiff{} wrong changes per pair, $p=\ptTerraHThreeRaw$). With \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, \casepath made \thLunaCPWrong{} wrong changes and \design{Rules in context} \thLunaRWrong{} ($p=\ptLunaHThreeRaw$ at its floor; Holm \ptLunaHThree{} within, \ptLunaHThreeHolmAll{} across ten). But \thLunaRWrongInShared{} of these \thLunaRWrong{} were errors in a story's unchanged case, repeated across that story's conditions. Counted once, \design{Rules in context} made \thLunaRDedupWrongPP{} wrong changes per pair against \casepath's \thLunaCPDedupWrongPP{} ($p=\ctLunaHThreeDedupRP$), and against majority voting the difference vanished (\ctLunaHThreeDedupRSCDiff, $p=\ctLunaHThreeDedupRSCP$).

\paragraph{Unresolved facts}
When the fact was left open, the designs almost never requested its documents: \design{Rules in context} with \texttt{gpt-6-luna} did so in \thLunaRPremature\% of pair runs, \casepath and every other model-written design never; only two-valued execution with open facts read as true did so often (Section~\ref{sec:results-uncertainty}). The designs differed in what else they changed. With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \design{Rules in context} left every request unchanged in \thTerraRStable\% of pair runs and \casepath in \thTerraCPStable\% (the difference favours \design{Rules in context}; $p=\ptTerraHFourRaw$ at its floor, Holm \ptTerraHFour); with \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, \casepath did so in \thLunaCPStable\% and \design{Rules in context} in \thLunaRStable\% ($p=\ptLunaHFourRaw$ at its floor; Holm \ptLunaHFour{} within, \ptLunaHFourHolmAll{} across ten). With six clusters, no H4 result can be stronger than these.

\paragraph{Across models}
Figure~\ref{fig:models} shows wrong changes per pair by model. Across the two current models and the original study's single run with \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra} (Appendix~\ref{app:original}), \casepath made between \rangeCPWrongPPLo{} and \rangeCPWrongPPHi{} wrong changes per pair and \design{Rules in context} between \rangeRWrongPPLo{} and \rangeRWrongPPHi; both upper ends come from that single run, and the \texttt{gpt-6-luna} value of \design{Rules in context} falls from \thLunaRWrongPP{} to \thLunaRDedupWrongPP{} when repeated errors count once. On the tenancy claims the pattern inverts: \design{Rules in context} requested \pct{\tenTerraBoneRecall} and \pct{\tenLunaBoneRecall} of required documents at once with the two models, \casepath \pct{\tenTerraCPxRecall} and \pct{\tenLunaCPxRecall}. Scoring every pairing of runs or only the seven conditions with agreed references changes none of these conclusions (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\subsection{Deferral and judgments: hypothesis H2}
\label{sec:results-uncertainty}

\paragraph{Outcomes}
With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \casepath requested \pct{\tenTerraCPxRecall} of required documents at once, held \pct{\tenTerraCPxHeld}, handed \pct{\tenTerraCPxHanded} over and missed \pct{\tenTerraCPxMiss}; \design{Rules in context} requested \pct{\tenTerraBoneRecall}, held \pct{\tenTerraBoneHeld} and missed \pct{\tenTerraBoneMiss} (Figure~\ref{fig:outcomes}). H2 was supported with both models. Strong Kleene evaluation in the production engine held far more (\pct{\tenTerraCPkHeld} and \pct{\tenLunaCPkHeld}); exact deferral removed \tenTerraExactCut\% and \tenLunaExactCut\% of its deferrals ($p=\ctTerraHTwoBDeferP$ and $p\ctLunaHTwoBDeferP$) without adding an unnecessary request. It added \tenTerraExactAddedMiss{} and \tenLunaExactAddedMiss{} missed documents among \tenTerraExactNR{} required ones: supplied files that \casepath had judged sufficient and the reference marks as insufficient, which the holds had hidden. \design{Model executes rules}, told to apply strong Kleene logic, held \pct{\tenTerraBtwoHeld} and so reproduced part of its incompleteness.

\paragraph{Judgments}
Of \jaTotal{} condition judgments on claims whose family \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra} identified, \jaCorrect{} matched the reference, \jaFlipped{} contradicted it and \jaUnres{} were unresolved; \texttt{gpt-6-luna} made \jaLunaCorrect, \jaLunaFlipped{} and \jaLunaUnres. Of the frontier model's unresolved judgments, \jaUnresFalse{} concern facts that the message never mentions and the scenario sets to false, and \jaUnresTrue{} concern true facts the message does not state clearly, \jaUnresRouting{} of them a routing fact. That one kind of fact holds most deferred documents: \defRouting{} of \defTotal{} (\defRoutingPct\%) with \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra} and \defLunaRouting{} of \defLunaTotal{} (\defLunaRoutingPct\%) with \texttt{gpt-6-luna}; with the frontier model, another \defClaim{} were handed over and \defOther{} waited for other conditions.

\paragraph{Unstated facts in the references}
The generator gives every unstated fact a value, so a design that requests a document whose requirement rests on such a fact is scored correct when its presumption matches the generator's, and a design that holds it is scored as deferring. Re-executing \casepath's recorded judgments with unresolved facts read as false, as the generator treats unmentioned facts, turned its holds into missed documents (\pct{\tvTerraClosedMiss} and \pct{\tvLunaClosedMiss} of required documents); reading them as true, as it treats routing facts, made \pct{\tvTerraEagerUnnec} and \pct{\tvLunaEagerUnnec} of requests unnecessary and, on the theft pairs, requested the open condition's documents prematurely in \tvThTerraEagerPremature\% and \tvThLunaEagerPremature\% of pair runs (post hoc). Holding a document that is not required goes unscored: \design{Rules in context} held \tenTerraBoneHoldsNonreq{} such documents per claim and \casepath \tenTerraCPxHoldsNonreq.

\paragraph{The running example}
On the theft variant in which the policyholder has not yet found the policy schedule, every design held the receipt for the watch in every run (\watchTerraDHeld, \watchTerraRHeld{} and \watchTerraCPHeld{} of \watchTerraRRuns{} runs for \design{Direct}, \design{Rules in context} and \casepath with \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}; \watchLunaRHeld{} and \watchLunaCPHeld{} with \texttt{gpt-6-luna}) and none requested it. The designs differed elsewhere. With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \design{Rules in context} changed nothing else in \watchTerraRStable{} of \watchTerraRRuns{} runs, \casepath in \watchTerraCPStable{} and \design{Direct} in \watchTerraDStable; \design{Direct} added the policy schedule itself or dropped the claim form, and \casepath added or withdrew the written assignment of claims against third parties, because its two calls judged an unrelated condition differently. With \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, \design{Rules in context} added or dropped a double-insurance declaration or added purchase vouchers in \the\numexpr\watchLunaRRuns-\watchLunaRStable\relax{} runs, and \casepath added the third-party assignment in \the\numexpr\watchLunaCPRuns-\watchLunaCPStable\relax.

\subsection{Rule errors}
\label{sec:results-rules}

With \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}, \ecWrongFaulty{} of \casepath's \thTerraCPWrong{} wrong changes and \ecMissedFaulty{} of its \thTerraCPMissed{} missed changes trace to five of the \rvRules{} extracted rules (Figure~\ref{fig:trace}): a route that demands a receipt and an expert valuation where the sources accept either, a route that adds a bank statement, an unconditional claim-form section, and two conditions broader than their sources. In the original study's single run, \origTraceWrongFive{} of \casepath's \origTraceWrong{} wrong changes and all \origTraceMissed{} traced misses came from the same five rules; the other \origTraceWrongRuleTwentyThree{} came from a sixth rule, on the formal expert procedure, the condition whose reference the re-derivation did not reproduce. A blind check of every extracted rule against its cited passages flagged all five and \the\numexpr\rvFlagged-\rvImplicatedFlagged\relax{} of the other \the\numexpr\rvRules-\rvImplicated\relax{} rules, but not the sixth.

Given the same rules, \design{Rules in context} with the frontier model repeated \ecFaultyAlsoR{} of these \ecWrongFaulty{} errors in the same pair and run and did not make the other \ecFaultyAvoidedR; its output does not link its decisions to rules. Its quotations were all verbatim (\citBone), as were those of \casepath's interpreter (\citCPProp); with \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, \pct{\citLunaBoneExact} and \pct{\citLunaCPPropExact} were, and \casepath's admission turns judgments with inexact quotations into unresolved ones. A quotation in a model-written plan supports a stated reason whose consistency with the rules is not checked; a quotation in \casepath supports a judgment from which each request follows by a rule. On the tenancy claims, whose rules are correct by construction, \casepath's \tenTerraCPxFP{} unnecessary requests follow from the \jaFlipped{} contradicting judgments, and its \tenTerraCPxMissN{} missed documents are supplied files it judged sufficient that the reference marks as insufficient (attributed by manual inspection).

\subsection{Agreement, verification, handoffs and cost: hypothesis H5}
\label{sec:results-cost}

\paragraph{Agreement across runs}
H5 was not supported; the difference went the other way. On the tenancy claims, \design{Rules in context} agreed more with itself across runs than \casepath (mean Jaccard \ctTerraHFiveB{} against \ctTerraHFiveA; \ctLunaHFiveB{} against \ctLunaHFiveA{} with \texttt{gpt-6-luna}). Execution is deterministic but its inputs are not, and the verifier varies most: without it, \casepath's agreement was \tenTerraCPxnRetest. On the theft pairs (secondary), \casepath agreed more with \texttt{gpt-6-luna} (\ctLunaHFiveThRA{} against \ctLunaHFiveThRB, $p=\ctLunaHFiveThRP$) and slightly less with \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra} (\ctTerraHFiveThRA{} against \ctTerraHFiveThRB, $p=\ctTerraHFiveThRP$).

\paragraph{The verifier}

\paragraph{Repairs and break-even (post hoc)}
Two repairs were evaluated on the recorded judgments; they were not confirmed in new runs. Admitting the family whenever the verifier affirms it leaves \repTerraHandoffRuns{} and \repLunaHandoffRuns{} handoff runs and the unsafe errors unchanged (\repTerraUnsafe{} and \repLunaUnsafe{} per claim); under the strict measure \casepath then makes \repTerraUnsafeStrict{} against \repTerraCtUnsafeStrictB{} with \texttt{gpt-6.1-terra} ($p=\repTerraCtUnsafeStrictP$) and \repLunaUnsafeStrict{} against \repLunaCtUnsafeStrictB{} with \texttt{gpt-6-luna} ($p=\repLunaCtUnsafeStrictP$). The break-even analysis asks how much an unsafe error must cost, in deferred documents, for \casepath to be preferable. With \texttt{gpt-6-luna}, it is preferable only if an unsafe error costs more than \beLunaCPxRatio{} deferred documents as run, \beLunaCPrRatio{} with the routing presumption, \repLunaRatio{} with the family repair and \repPresLunaRatio{} with both; under the strict measure, no ratio favours it without the repair, and the thresholds become \repLunaRatioStrict{} and \repPresLunaRatioStrict{} with it.

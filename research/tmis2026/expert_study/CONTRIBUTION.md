# Contribution statement

## In the combined TMIS paper (formulation, outside experiments, expert study)

1. The paper states a formulation of evidence-requesting agents in which a language model judges each rule condition as true, false or unresolved with a quotation, and code executes the organisation's rules, so that the agent's uncertainty is explicit and its errors are traceable to a judgment or a rule.
2. Pre-registered experiments outside the insurer, on synthetic claims with a fixed product commit, compare the product with a frontier model given the same rules and with prompting methods from prior work. (Provisional: the result clause is written from the confirmatory test, which has not run; the development measurements so far show no advantage on small rule sets and an advantage where many rules are in force at once.)
3. The expert study then puts the same frozen product (same commit and workflow format by the parity manifest, the insurer's model and process data as the only differences) in front of five claims experts of a legal-expenses insurer, on three real cases cut at a decision point. It is a field evaluation whose only instrument is the experts' ordinary work in the product: they keep, change with a reason, add, or note. It reports the agreement of CasePath, and of a single-prompt comparator, with what the insurer actually did at that point and later; this is agreement with practice, not accuracy.
4. It reports what five experts do with the same stored items (their agreement per item, and the items most of them change) and reliance on document requests, the one item kind with a status independent of the product's result: whether experts add what practice requested and the product lacked, and remove what practice never requested more often than what it did request. Facts, deadlines and document status have no independent status here, and no reliance claim is made about them.
5. It tests whether corrections improve agreement with practice on a case whose corrections the system has not seen, by model-free replay with a pre-registered mechanical rule from correction to Knowledge proposal and a nested gate that never sees the held-out case, so that "learning from corrections" is a measured, falsifiable claim rather than a design promise.

## If the expert study is published on its own

6. On its own the study is a pre-registered field evaluation of human review of an agentic case-preparation system in a regulated insurance process, without adjudicators or questionnaires: agreement with practice for the product and a single-prompt comparator, five experts' agreement on the same stored items, reliance on document requests against practice, the reasons experts give, and learning by replay with a nested gate. It makes no claim about appropriate reliance in general, trust, workload or effort saving.
7. Its inference is conditional on three cases, one insurer, one process and five experts; it supports estimates with intervals and one pre-stated test, not population claims.

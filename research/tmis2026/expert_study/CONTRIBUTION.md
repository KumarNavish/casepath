# Contribution statement

## In the combined TMIS paper (formulation, outside experiments, expert study)

1. The paper states a formulation of evidence-requesting agents in which a language model judges each rule condition as true, false or unresolved with a quotation, and code executes the organisation's rules, so that the agent's uncertainty is explicit and its errors are traceable to a judgment or a rule.
2. Pre-registered experiments outside the insurer, on synthetic claims with a fixed product commit, compare the product with a frontier model given the same rules and with prompting methods from prior work. (Provisional: the result clause is written from the confirmatory test, which has not run; the development measurements so far show no advantage on small rule sets and an advantage where many rules are in force at once.)
3. The expert study then tests the remedy the experiments point to, review by people, with the same frozen product (same commit and workflow format by the parity manifest, the insurer's model and process data as the only differences): five claims experts of a legal-expenses insurer review the same stored output on three real cases, and every item they keep, change or add is scored against gold made by two adjudicators.
4. It reports appropriate reliance at the level of single assertions of an agent, separating what experts checked from what they only had on screen, and estimates anchoring from each expert's own answer before and after seeing the agent's result.
5. It tests whether corrections improve the system on a case it has not seen, with a pre-registered mechanical rule from correction to rule proposal and a safety gate that never sees the held-out case, so that "learning from corrections" is a measured, falsifiable claim rather than a design promise.

## If the expert study is published on its own

6. On its own the study is a pre-registered field evaluation of human review of an agentic case-preparation system in a regulated insurance process, with item-level gold, a single-prompt comparator and a taxonomy of correction reasons tested for overlap by blind coding.
7. Its inference is conditional on three cases, one insurer and five experts; it supports estimates with intervals and two pre-stated tests, not population claims.

# Scales and the stimulated-recall guide

Every German wording below was copied from the source named beside it, with that source open. Before use, a second
person checks the copy against the same source.

## 1. Scales: raw NASA-TLX in German (five items) and two subscales of the Trust in Automation questionnaire (five items)

Ten distinct items in total: five workload items and five trust items.

### 1.1 Workload: NASA-TLX, German version of Unema et al. (1988), raw form

The Unema et al. version is chosen because its origin is the oldest documented German version and its complete wording
and response format can be copied from an open, archived source with the attribution printed beside it. Flägel et al.
(2019) has the better published psychometric evaluation, but its wording could not be opened; it is cited only as
evidence that a German TLX behaves as one factor in professional work (abstract only).
A third, web version (keithv.com/software/nasatlx/nasatlx_german.html, "German translation by Christian Lehsing and
Katharina Seifert") differs from Seifert (2002) in a few words, for example "geistige Anforderung" for "geistige
Anstrengung" in item 1 and "Leistung" for "Ausführung der Aufgaben". It is not used: its origin is given only by the
translators' names.

**Validation.** Unema et al. (1988) report first results with the German version; **not opened** (a congress volume, no
open copy found; a person must obtain it through a library, GfA Jahresdokumentation 1988). The only validation evidence
opened is in Seifert (2002), Anhang 1 (printed p. 142): the German TLX total score correlated r = .78 with the SEA scale (N = 72). With
five experts no psychometric property is estimated here.

**Original instrument.** Hart, S. G. & Staveland, L. E. (1988). Development of NASA-TLX (Task Load Index): Results of
empirical and theoretical research. In P. A. Hancock & N. Meshkati (Eds.), *Human Mental Workload* (pp. 139–183).
North-Holland. Cited for the instrument; the German wording does not depend on it.

**Items used, copied from Seifert (2002), Anhang 5, "Beanspruchungshöhe", pp. 152–153.** Line breaks and the
hyphenation of the print are removed; nothing else is changed (including "ihrer Meinung" in lower case, as printed).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Geistige Anforderungen | Wie viel geistige Anstrengung war bei der Informationsaufnahme und bei der Informationsverarbeitung erforderlich (z.B. Dropped: "Körperliche Anforderungen".

**Instruction, as printed (p. 152):** "Geben Sie jetzt bitte an, wie hoch die Beanspruchung in den einzelnen Dimensionen
war. Markieren sie dazu auf den folgenden Skalen bitte, in welchem Maße Sie sich in den sechs genannten Dimensionen von
der Aufgabe beansprucht oder gefordert gesehen haben:"

**Response format, as printed.** A horizontal line with 21 tick marks (20 equal intervals; longer ticks at both ends and
in the middle), end labels as in the table, one mark per item. Scoring: each tick is a value from 0 to 100 in steps of
5, left end 0 (for item 3, "gut" = 0, so that a higher value is always more workload). The 0–100 coding is the usual
TLX convention and is not printed in Seifert (2002); it is stated here as our coding.

**Raw form.** The source contains the pairwise weighting part ("Beanspruchungsstruktur", pp. 149–151). It is not used.
The score is the unweighted mean of the five items (0–100).

**Deviations from the source, disclosed in the paper.** (1) Physical demand is dropped: reviewing a case on screen has
no physical demand, and the item would add noise and time. (2) Because one item is dropped, "sechs" in the instruction
becomes "fünf". (3) The weighting part is not used (raw form). (4) The scale is shown on screen; a click on a tick sets
the value.

### 1.2 Trust: Trust in Automation questionnaire (TiA), subscales "Trust in Automation" and "Propensity to Trust"

**Why not FOST.** The five German FOST items (Franke et al. 2015) could not be found in any source that could be opened:
no open copy of the paper and no author distribution was found (to use FOST, a person must request the German items
and their source from the authors). The recommendation is therefore switched to TiA, whose
author distributes German and English versions openly (CC BY-SA 4.0).

**Sources, all opened.**
- Questionnaire, German: `Vertrauen-in-Automation_TiA_Fragebogen.pdf`, author's repository
  (raw.githubusercontent.com/moritzkoerber/tia_trust_in_automation_questionnaire/master/), identical in text to
  `2020-11-29_Vertrauen-in-Automation_TiA_Fragebogen.pdf` on the author's OSF project (osf.io/y3jn5). Footer:
  "Fragebogen „Vertrauen in Automation" (TiA) | Moritz Körber, Technische Universität München".
- Item-to-subscale key and reversed items: `Items_and_scales.pdf`, same OSF project.
- Development and validation: Körber, M. (2019). Theoretical considerations and development of a questionnaire to
  measure trust in automation. In S. Bagnara et al. (Eds.), *Proceedings of the 20th Congress of the International
  Ergonomics Association (IEA 2018), Vol. VI* (pp. 13–30). Springer. Author's preprint on the same OSF project.

**What is used and why this subset.** TiA has 19 items in six subscales (Reliability/Competence 6,
Understanding/Predictability 4, Familiarity 2, Intention of Developers 2, Propensity to Trust 3, Trust in Automation 2).
The author treats the subscales as separately scored scales: the preprint reports the two-item subscale Trust in
Automation and the subscale Competence analysed on their own in Körber, Baseler & Bengler (2018), the two-item subscale
"was more sensitive regarding group differences and performed equally as well as the full TiA questionnaire regarding
all other measures", and the repository's README discusses a shortened "core questionnaire" and says whether a total
score is meaningful is still open. We use two whole subscales and no total score:

- **Trust in Automation** (items 9 and 14; two items): trust in CasePath itself; asked twice.
- **Propensity to Trust** (items 5, 12, 18; three items; α = .75 in the first validation sample, per the preprint): the
  expert's general disposition, used to read the two trust values; asked once.

Reliability/Competence with Trust in Automation (the pair the author analysed) would need eight trust items and break
the ten-item limit; Understanding/Predictability would need six. Familiarity and Intention of Developers are the two
subscales the README marks for possible removal.

**Items, copied from the German questionnaire, in the questionnaire's order and numbering.**

| No. in TiA | Subscale | German (as printed) | Reversed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Propensity to Trust | Bei unbekannten automatisierten Systemen sollte man eher vorsichtig sein. | yes |
| 9 | Trust in Automation | Ich vertraue dem System. | no |
| 12 | Propensity to Trust | Ich vertraue einem System eher, als dass ich ihm misstraue. | no |
| 14 | Trust in Automation | Ich kann mich auf das System verlassen. | no |
| 18 | Propensity to Trust | Automatisierte Systeme funktionieren generell gut. | no |

**Response format, as printed.** Five points with labels: (1) Stimme gar nicht zu, (2) Stimme eher nicht zu, (3) Stimme
weder zu noch nicht zu, (4) Stimme eher zu, (5) Stimme voll zu; plus "Keine Angabe". Scoring: subscale mean of items
answered (1–5), item 5 reversed (6 − value); "Keine Angabe" is missing. The two subscales are reported separately.

**Deviations, disclosed in the paper.** (1) Four of six subscales are not used. (2) "Das System" is introduced once, in
the instruction, as CasePath. (3) Propensity to Trust is asked once, the Trust in Automation subscale twice. (4) The
English version, if quoted in the paper, is the author's English version, which the README says is not yet validated.

### 1.3 When and how long

| Scale | When | Time |
|---|---|---|
| TLX, five items | after each of the three study cases | about 1 minute each |
| TiA Propensity to Trust, three items | once, after the practice case | under 1 minute |
| TiA Trust in Automation, two items | after the practice case, and after case 3 before the recall conversation | under 1 minute each |

Total time for all scales: about five minutes.

**Not claimed.** With five experts, no psychometric property of either scale is estimated here; values are reported
per case and per expert (unlabelled), descriptively. The TiA Trust in Automation subscale has two items; the preprint
reports α between .63 and .85 for it across three occasions, so single values are read with care.

## 2. Stimulated-recall guide (twenty minutes)

### 2.1 What is shown

Selection, fixed in advance and computed from the record when case 3 closes:

1. **All of the expert's own corrections, dismissals and additions** on the three cases, in the order made. If there are more than twelve, the twelve are drawn at random, stratified by reason, with a recorded seed.
2. **Three kept items** (never changed by this expert): every kept chain item that gold labels incorrect, ordered by total on-screen time (longest first), then kept correct chain items with the longest on-screen time until there are three. The kept items are mixed among the corrections and shown the same way.

**Whether the expert is told which kept items were wrong.** Not during the conversation, because it would turn the
remaining explanations into justifications, and not at the end of the session, because experts could pass it on
before the others have had their sessions. After the last of the five sessions, all five experts are shown together
which kept items the reference judged incorrect, for all three cases, without naming who kept what (settled on
2026-11-10).

Each item is shown on the expert's own screen state as it was when they acted (from the event log): the item, its
evidence, and the before and after values. The tapped reason is **hidden** from the researcher's view and from the
expert.

### 2.2 Fixed opening (read aloud)

> "Thank you. We will now look at some places in the three cases where you did something, or left something as it
> was. There are no right or wrong answers here; we want to understand how you work, so that CasePath can be improved.
> For each place, please tell me what you were thinking at that moment. If you do not remember, say so; that is also
> useful."

### 2.3 Neutral prompts

Use only these, in this order, and no others:

1. "What were you thinking here?"
2. "What did you look at to decide?"
3. "What would have had to be different for you to decide otherwise?"
4. (only for a correction) "Where does the right information come from?"

Do not: name a reason category, say whether CasePath or the expert was right, praise, or ask "why didn't you…".
Silence of up to ten seconds is allowed before moving on.

### 2.4 What is recorded

- Audio, inside the insurer only; transcribed inside; the transcript belongs to the content export.
- Per item: `event_id` (or `item_id` for a kept item), start and end of the segment (relative time), whether the expert remembered.
- No video, no screen recording beyond what the event log already holds.

### 2.5 Coding (blind)

1. A researcher who did not run the session codes each explanation with one of the six reasons (1 Steht anders in den Akten; 2 Steht nicht in den Akten; 3 Hier nicht relevant; 4 Gilt generell so; 5 Weiss ich von ausserhalb der Akten; 6 Formulierung) or "not codable", from the transcript only, without the tapped reason, the item's gold label or the expert's identity.
2. A short coding manual is written before the first session: one paragraph per reason with the remedy it points to, and a rule for ties (the reason pointing to the earliest stage of the chain: reading before checking before process condition before rule before wording).
3. Agreement with the tapped reason: Cohen's kappa with a bootstrap interval, on corrections where both exist; the confusion table; the share "ohne Angabe" among tapped reasons, and how those were coded.
4. A second coder codes a random 30 % for coder agreement (exploratory).
5. For kept items, the explanation is coded as: checked and agreed; did not check; trusted CasePath; not relevant to the task; other (exploratory).

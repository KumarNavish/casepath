# Review of the feedback and study design

Reviewed: `docs/FEEDBACK_DESIGN.md`, `docs/HANDLER_BAR.md` and `docs/PARITY.md` of the product extract at commit
`6f3de061`, against the product code at that commit. Code locations are relative to `casepath-internal/casepath/`.
Rows marked **[added]** are weaknesses not in the planner's list. Rows are ordered by how much of the study fails if
the weakness stays.

The model is stochastic, so five experts on "the same system" see different items. | "Five judgments per item" is false unless all five see the same item. | Produce each case's run once before session 1. ## Notes

**Where I differ from the planner's points.**
- *Point 5, the relative reliance measures.* Schemmer et al. (2023, arXiv 2302.02187v3, equations 1 and 2) define relative AI reliance (RAIR) and relative self-reliance (RSR) on decisions where a person first decides alone, then sees advice, then decides again. RAIR counts only instances where the initial decision was wrong and the advice correct, and gives the share of them whose final decision is correct; RSR counts only instances where the initial decision was correct and the advice wrong, and gives the share whose final decision is correct. Instances where both were right or both wrong enter neither. Here that holds only for the unaided case, and only for its decision units (process step, each catalogue document requested or not, next action). That is five expert-case pairs, each with a handful of units, and few of them will fall into either denominator. The measures fit in form but will have denominators near zero. Report them as counts with their denominators, never as rates alone.
- *Point 5, position of the unaided case.* I recommend the unaided case always come first after the practice case, not rotated. The unaided answers carry the anchoring estimate (point 8), and any aided case before them teaches what CasePath lists. Position is then confounded with condition, which is acceptable because the effort comparison is descriptive. The allocation table in the pre-registration does this; the rotated alternative was rejected (settled on 2026-11-10).
- *Analysis frame, bootstrap over experts.* See row 14a.
- *Ranking.* Changes that must exist before session 1 come first. The event log (row 2) is not in the planner's top five, but without it Q2 cannot be computed at all, and it cannot be added later. The comparator (row 8) can be produced and labelled after the sessions, from stored inputs, with no expert time; it moves to sixth place.

**Use in one paper or two.** The study is planned for the TMIS paper. The pre-registration is self-contained and versioned, and the event fields keep the raw sequence, exposure intervals, every item version and each adjudicator's labels, so a separate later paper loses nothing (`EVENT_FIELDS.md`, section 8).

**What the design does well.** One unit (the item) for product and research; reasons chosen by remedy; the fifth reason, which separates CasePath's errors from what it could not know; model-free evaluation; the parity manifest; content-free exports with a guard (`quality/scorecard.py`). These should stay.

**What the product already has.** Stable keys for fact, document, deadline, open point, letter clause and timeline items (`web/app.py:71`); the edit operations that an addition needs (`process/casegraph.py:17`); model-free evaluation of a stored result under any Knowledge and edits (`agents/core.py:366`, `evaluate`); model-free rescoring of stored benchmark runs (`quality/evaluate.py:432`); the parity manifest; the scorecard guard. A replay command that applies a Knowledge version to stored runs does not exist yet.

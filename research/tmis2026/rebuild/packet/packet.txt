Costed plan for the fixes and Study 4 (gate 1, for Navish, 2026-11-13)
Nothing below has been spent. Costs are model credits in USD from a cost model built on the stored ledgers (rebuild/plan/COST_
MODEL.md, STUDY4_COST.md; the design is data in study4_cost.py). Every figure for files longer than today’s (575 words, four attachments) is an extrapolation; the low figure is the central estimate, the high one adds 25% and lets output grow with length. Work by writing
and checking sessions costs no credits.
1. What the figure drafts found, and what it changes
1. The hold can be taught to a prompt. On Study 3’s sealed tenancy claims the prompt that may hold holds 97 of 100 requests that depend
on an open fact; CasePath’s core holds 99.6 on the cost-efficient model and 87 on the frontier model. The difference today is on the other
side: of 100 requests the file settles, that prompt holds or drops 10, the core 4, and the core’s needless holds sit on claims that deny their
event. The paper’s first question is answered by all columns of Figure 4, not by one.
2. “Nothing else moves” is not true yet. One changed sentence moved exactly one line in 12 of 144 stored theft pair-runs of the core; in
130 another line moved too, because readings of other facts differ between runs. The same theft text gave the same checklist in all three
runs for 8 of 128 texts (tenancy: 70 of 96).
3. Three readings are not better than one. In the core, one reading gives 0.55 deviations per tenancy claim, three give 0.74: the extra
readings add needless holds.
4. Cost is not in the re-reading. Reading again only where the first reading does not settle a fact would save 6 to 7% per file. 62% of the
core’s cost is the judging of supplied documents, and each of about ten calls carries the whole file. On a 2,500-word file the core as tested
would cost about 40 times the prompt that may hold.
5. After a rule edit, the stored readings are enough. One edited line changed 15 of 421 stored checklist lines with no model call, 14 of them
exactly as the edited rule requires. Read again without any edit, CasePath’s checklist lines differ as often as a prompt’s (about 6 against
7%): the gain is from not reading again.

3. The benchmark, extended (no credits)
• Length: three levels: about 330 words with zero to four attachments (today’s), 800 words with three attachments, 2,500 words with six
attachments, a fifth of them scanned.
• Rules: the same files are read with the rules of one regime, of three regimes and of four regimes in one catalogue.
• Open facts: share of files with an open fact 0, 10, 25 and 50%.
• Twins: for some files a second file one sentence apart.
• A fourth regime, added from public text by a session that has not seen the system, following the six steps; its hours and sessions are
recorded.
• About 930 sealed files are written by a model family that is not evaluated and checked blind, as before.
4. Study 4 (one registered test, after a freeze with an external timestamp)
• Cells: three regimes × two models × five designs (CasePath’s core; the prompt that may hold; self-consistency at CasePath’s cost; the
model executing the rules; ReAct). The main table shows every cell.
• Files per regime: 160 for the open-fact sweep (40 per level, short); 40 medium and 40 long at the 25% level; the 40 short files of that
level read again with the larger catalogues; 30 twins; 20 files run ten times on the cost-efficient model and three times on the frontier
model. Fourth regime: 120 short files.
• Measures: all deviations per file against the clean-room reference (a needless hold is an error); the share of files with the same checklist
in every run; cost per file.
• Rule change: three edits per regime. For every design: lines changed as the edit requires, lines changed that it does not reach, model
calls. One edit adds a fact, so CasePath’s cost for that case is measured too.
• Hypotheses, per model, frozen in order before the run: at the hard end of each axis (long files, four regimes, half the files open) CasePath
makes fewer errors than the strongest prompt; at the easy end it makes no more (margin 0.15 per file); after the rule edits it changes
more of the right lines, fewer of the wrong ones, with fewer calls. A lost cell is reported as lost.

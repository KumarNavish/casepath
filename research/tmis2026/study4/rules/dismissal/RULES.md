# Rule set: dismissal (version 2026-11-12.5)

Development material for Study 4, step 2a. Every clause cites the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO, SR 220, consolidated version applicable from 2026-10-01) or the Civil Procedure Code (CPC, SR 272, version applicable from 2026-07-01), both from Fedlex. Each passage is quoted in the English translation with the German text, which is authoritative, beside it (`sources/*.txt`). A document is needed when its step is reached and its condition holds. Reconciliation with the second reader: `RECONCILIATION.md`.

## Where the translation was not followed

The German text is authoritative. Where the English translation differs from it in legal meaning, the rules follow the German:

- CO art. 336b para. 2: the English says “The claim prescribes if not brought before the courts”; the German says „eine Klage anhängig gemacht ... ist der Anspruch verwirkt“. The rules read it as forfeited ("verwirkt"), which applies by itself, not "prescribes" (a time bar that must be pleaded); the claim must be made pending ("anhängig gemacht"), which under CPC art. 62 para. 1 happens when a conciliation request or an action is filed, read with CPC art. 62 para. 1 (an inference across the two acts: the CO says "anhängig gemacht", the CPC defines "Rechtshängigkeit"), not only "brought before the courts". (Used in: action_in_time, b_stop_time_limit, court_filing.)
- CO art. 336 para. 2 let. b: the English says “the employer cannot cite just cause”; the German says „der Arbeitgeber nicht beweisen kann, dass er einen begründeten Anlass zur Kündigung hatte“. The rules read it as the employer must prove a justified reason ("begründeter Anlass"), a lower standard than the good cause ("wichtiger Grund") of art. 337. (Used in: b_representative.)
- CO art. 336 para. 1: the English says “Notice of termination is unlawful”; the German says „Die Kündigung ... ist missbräuchlich“. The rules read it as abusive ("missbräuchlich"): the notice stays effective and gives rise to compensation (arts. 336a, 336b), it is not void. (Used in: b_grounds, type 2 summary.)
- CO art. 336b para. 1: the English says “submit his objection ... in writing to the party giving such notice”; the German says „beim Kündigenden schriftlich Einsprache erheben“. The rules read it as the objection is lodged with the employer, not merely sent. (Used in: objection_submitted, written_objection.)

## Known behaviour

- When the illness fact (`incapacity_illness_accident`, type 1) is open, the proof of incapacity (`incapacity_proof`) is held behind the question, not requested: the fact carries the 30, 90 or 180-day limit of art. 336c para. 1 let. b, which a lay claim often leaves unsettled, so the illness branch shows more holds than the other two protected periods (second reader, 2026-11-12).
- In type 2, `written_objection` and `court_filing` are needed only when their own fact is yes; when that fact is unmentioned or open they are held behind the question (the claimant knows whether they objected or filed), and when it is stated no nothing is requested and the stop applies.
- Silence never ends a process, but it can choose which stop ends it: with 180 days passed, no claim made pending and `agreed_to_continue` unmentioned, the convention's no leads to `b_stop_time_limit`, where yes would lead to `b_stop_continued`. This follows from the convention (SPEC.md 6a).

### Presumptions

None from statute: no cited passage says that an unmentioned fact is absent.

Convention of this benchmark (not law), not a passage of law: A fact that the file does not mention is taken as not alleged, and the rules are applied as if it were absent. Silence never ends a process: where a fact's absence would end it, the unmentioned fact is unresolved. The defining event is never presumed: if the file does not mention it, it is unresolved. A fact that the file raises and leaves unsettled is open.

Filled with no under the convention: `notice_during_probation`, `probation_varied`, `incapacity_illness_accident`, `compulsory_service`, `pregnancy_or_birth`, `notice_period_varied`. No presumption because false would end the process: none. Always stated: `notice_before_protected_period`. Defining event `employer_gave_notice`: never presumed.

# Tenancy rules: which documents to request

These rules come from the Swiss Code of Obligations (SR 220), Title Eight
(Leases), consolidated version of 1 October 2026. Article numbers refer to
that Code. They apply to a residential tenant's dispute that falls into one of
three types: a defect of the rented dwelling, a termination of the lease by the
landlord, or a rent increase.

How to apply them:

- Each type has deciding questions. Answer each from the claim and its
  attachments: yes, no, open (the claim raises the matter and leaves it
  unsettled), or not stated.
- A document is requested when its step is reached and its condition holds.
- No answer is assumed. A question the claim does not answer stays unresolved,
  and so does every step or document that depends on it.
- If the claim states that the type's defining event did not happen, the
  process ends and no document is requested.

## 1. Defect of the rented dwelling (mould, damp, heating)

Deciding questions:

1. Defining event (art. 259a para. 1): Does the claim state that the rented dwelling has a defect (for example mould, damp, or heating that fails) or that the tenant is disturbed in using the dwelling as agreed, without the claim itself saying that the tenant caused the defect or must remedy it as minor cleaning or repairs?
   This does not cover a defect the claim itself attributes to the tenant, or
   minor cleaning or repairs the tenant must do (art. 259). A landlord's
   allegation that the tenant caused the defect, which the claim disputes, does
   not make the answer no.
2. (art. 257g para. 1) Does the claim state that the tenant has already informed the landlord of the defect?
   A tenant who only intends to inform the landlord has not informed.
3. (art. 259e) Does the tenant claim compensation from the landlord for damage the defect caused to the tenant (for example damaged furniture or clothing, or extra costs)?
   A rent reduction (art. 259d) is not compensation for damage.
4. (art. 259a para. 2) Does the claim state that the tenant intends to pay the rent on deposit with the office designated by the canton instead of to the landlord, or has begun to do so?
   Merely withholding or reducing rent, or the rental security paid at the
   start of the lease (art. 257e), is not a deposit.
5. (art. 259h para. 1) Does the claim state that at least one rent payment has already been paid into deposit with the office designated by the canton?
   An announced or planned deposit is not enough.

Stop: if the answer to question 1 is no, the process ends (art. 259a para. 1:
the tenant's rights arise only where such a defect arises or the agreed use is
disturbed).

Steps and documents, once question 1 is yes:

- Establish the defect and the agreed use (art. 256 para. 1: the landlord must
  keep the dwelling fit for its designated use). Request:
  - **Lease contract**: parties, rented dwelling, agreed use (art. 256 para. 1).
  - **Record of the defect (dated photographs, inspection or expert report)**:
    the defect and where it is, the date observed, how the use is affected
    (art. 259a para. 1).
- If question 2 is yes: establish when the landlord learned of the defect
  (art. 259b). Request **Tenant's notice of the defect to the landlord**:
  defect reported, addressee, date (art. 259d: a rent reduction runs from the
  time the landlord became aware).
- If question 3 is yes: establish the damage (art. 259e). Request **Proof of the damage (invoices, receipts, photographs of damaged items)**:
  damaged items or costs, amount, connection to the defect.
- If question 4 is yes: check the preconditions for paying rent on deposit
  (art. 259g para. 1: the tenant must announce the deposit in writing).
  Request **Tenant's written time limit to the landlord and written announcement of the deposit**:
  repair requested, time limit set, announcement of the deposit, date and
  addressee (art. 259g para. 1: the time limit must be set in writing).
  - If question 5 is also yes, request **Receipt of the deposit office for the deposited rent**:
    amount, date of the first deposit, rent period covered (art. 259h para. 1:
    claims must be brought within 30 days of the due date of the first
    deposited rent).

## 2. Termination of the lease by the landlord

Deciding questions:

1. Defining event (art. 273 para. 1): Has the tenant received a notice from the landlord terminating the lease of the dwelling?
   This does not cover a lease of limited duration that ends on its agreed date
   without notice (art. 266), a termination only announced or threatened, or a
   notice given by the tenant.
2. (art. 266n) Does the claim state that the tenant is married or in a registered partnership and that the rented dwelling serves as the family home?
   A partner who is neither spouse nor registered partner does not count.
3. (art. 257d para. 2) Does the notice or the claim state that the landlord terminated because the tenant did not pay overdue rent or accessory charges within a time limit the landlord had set?
   Arrears mentioned while the notice gives another reason do not count.
4. (art. 271a para. 1 let. a) Does the claim state that the landlord gave notice because the tenant had, in good faith, asserted claims under the lease (for example demanded repair of a defect or contested the rent)?
   Earlier claims the claim does not connect with the notice do not count.
5. (art. 272 para. 1) Does the tenant seek an extension of the lease because having to leave by the end date would be a hardship for the tenant or the tenant's family?
   Contesting the notice without asking for more time does not count.

Stop: if the answer to question 1 is no, the process ends (art. 273 para. 1:
the challenge runs from receipt of a notice of termination; with no notice
there is nothing to challenge).

Steps and documents, once question 1 is yes:

- Check the form of the notice, the notice period and the challenge deadline
  (art. 266o: a notice that does not conform to arts. 266l–266n is void).
  Request:
  - **Lease contract**: parties, dwelling, duration, agreed notice periods and
    termination dates (art. 266a para. 1).
  - **Landlord's notice of termination or written threat of termination**:
    sender and addressee, date, termination date, whether the form approved by
    the canton is used (art. 266l para. 2).
  - **Proof of the date the notice was received**: date of receipt and how it
    was delivered (art. 273 para. 1: a challenge must be brought within 30 days
    of receipt).
- If question 2 is yes: check separate service on the spouse or registered
  partner (art. 266n). Request **Copy of the notice served separately on the spouse or registered partner**,
  including, where the notice followed a payment time limit, the separately
  served copy of that time limit.
- If question 3 is yes: check the payment time limit that preceded the notice
  (art. 257d para. 1). Request **Landlord's written payment time limit with warning of termination**:
  amount in arrears, time limit (at least 30 days for a dwelling), warning of
  termination, date of receipt (art. 257d para. 1; the German text requires the
  time limit in writing).
- In every case: check whether the notice can be challenged as contrary to
  good faith (art. 271 para. 1). If question 4 is yes, request **Tenant's earlier correspondence asserting claims under the lease**:
  the claim asserted, date, addressee (art. 271a para. 1 let. a).
- If question 5 is yes and question 3 is no: assess an extension (art. 272a
  para. 1 let. a excludes an extension where notice was given for arrears).
  Request **Evidence of the tenant's personal, family and financial circumstances**:
  household members, financial circumstances, personal or health circumstances
  (art. 272 para. 2 let. c).

## 3. Rent increase

Deciding questions:

1. Defining event (art. 270b para. 1): Does the claim state that the landlord has communicated to the tenant an increase of the rent of the dwelling (whether or not on an official form)?
   This does not cover a planned increase not yet communicated, the initial
   rent of a new lease (art. 270), or new accessory charges or reduced services
   without a rent increase (art. 269d para. 3).
2. (art. 270d) Does the claim state that the increase is a step fixed in advance in the lease, by which the rent rises periodically by fixed amounts (graduated rent)?
   An index-linked increase is not a graduated step.
3. (art. 270c) Does the claim state that the increase applies a clause of the lease linking the rent to an index (such as the Swiss consumer price index)?
   An increase justified by inflation or costs without an index clause does not
   count.
4. (art. 269a let. b) Does the increase notice give, as its reason, higher costs for the landlord (for example higher mortgage interest or operating costs) or additional services or works by the landlord (for example a renovation)?
   An increase justified only by local customary rents (art. 269a let. a) or by
   inflation on risk capital (let. e) does not count.
5. (art. 269d para. 2 let. c) Does the claim state that, together with the rent increase, the landlord gave notice of termination or threatened to terminate the lease?
   A notice of termination given separately at another time does not count.

Stop: if the answer to question 1 is no, the process ends (art. 270b para. 1:
the challenge attaches to a communicated increase).

Steps and documents, once question 1 is yes:

- Check the form, reasons and timing of the notice (art. 269d para. 1: notice
  with reasons, on a form approved by the canton, at least ten days before the
  notice period begins). Request:
  - **Lease contract**: parties, dwelling, termination dates, any
    graduated-rent or index clause (art. 269d para. 1: an increase takes effect
    from the next termination date).
  - **Landlord's rent increase notice**: old and new rent, effective date,
    reasons, whether the approved form is used (art. 269d para. 2 let. a–b: void
    without the form or without reasons).
  - **Proof of the date the notice was received**: date of receipt and how it
    was delivered (art. 269d para. 1: at least ten days before the notice period).
- Then exactly one of:
  - if question 2 is yes: check the graduated-rent agreement (art. 269c: at
    least three years, at most one increase a year, amounts fixed in francs);
    no further document (art. 270d: graduated increases cannot be challenged);
  - if question 2 is no and question 3 is yes: check the index clause (art.
    269b: at least five years, Swiss consumer price index); no further document
    (art. 270c: only the change in the index can be argued);
  - if questions 2 and 3 are no: check whether the increase is justified by its
    reasons (art. 270b para. 1). If question 4 is yes, request **Landlord's breakdown of the cost increases or additional services relied on**:
    costs or works, amounts, share passed on to the rent (art. 269a let. b).
- If question 5 is yes: check the accompanying termination (art. 269d para. 2
  let. c: the increase is void). Request **Landlord's notice of termination or written threat of termination**:
  sender and addressee, date, the termination or threat, its connection to the
  increase.

CLAIM: constitutive_verbatim_substring_gate_primary_legal_source_to_downstream_artifacts
PREEMPTED: True

STRONGEST PREEMPTION:
 Jeff Wang, "A Registry-Bound LLM Pipeline for Evidence-Grounded Trait Extraction" (arXiv:2606.00994, June 2026) — substring-correspondence as an admission gate into a persisted registry, failures discarded at extraction time rather than flagged, check recurring across pipeline layers. This is the same OPERATION, minus the legal domain. It is strongest in a pincer with Anthropic's Citations API (Jan 2025), which makes "verbatim by construction, not by post-hoc check" a shipped commodity ~20 months before submission, and Ovcharov et al. (arXiv:2606.00898), which already owns "does the cited provision exist at a date t" as a named, decomposed problem in law.

RESIDUAL NOVELTY:
 Three things survive even granting the full preemption, and the paper should be rewritten around them rather than around "constitutive not diagnostic," which is dead.

1. THE TERNARY PREDICATE (strongest survivor). Prior work tests substring(quote, supplied_document) with the document's identity unexamined. The surviving operation is: discard unless THERE EXISTS a consolidation c of the NAMED authority A such that the span is a verbatim substring of hash-pinned passage(A, c). This binds text, authority, and version. It has a failure mode — "verbatim of something retrieved, but the named authority exists at no consolidation" — that CHyD, Anthropic Citations, Deterministic Quoting and Wang are structurally incapable of detecting, and that Ovcharov detects only post-hoc and only for identifiers, never for language. The join of quote-verification and authority-existence-over-version-space, enforced constitutively, is unoccupied. Frame the contribution as source-identity binding, not as gating.

2. THE ARTIFACT-LEVEL INVARIANT. One admissibility rule keyed to a shared passage hash across three semantically distinct persisted layers yields a property no prior system claims: the finished artifact is independently re-verifiable offline against the primary source, without the authoring model, by hash. Wang repeats a check across stages of one task; that is not the same object. This survives only if stated as a closure property and demonstrated — re-verify a shipped artifact end-to-end and report it — not asserted.

3. THE LEGAL INSTANTIATION AS EVIDENCE, NOT AS CONTRIBUTION. Versioned primary law is the one domain where the ternary predicate is well-posed, because consolidation identity is externally defined rather than invented. This makes the domain the right testbed; it does not make the domain the novelty.

WHAT MUST BE ADDED OR THE RESIDUAL DIES ANYWAY. Windisch has already established in print that an exact-substring gate is necessary-not-sufficient and costs coverage, so: report the discard rate at each of the three layers; report what fraction of discarded items were genuinely unsupported versus false discards from normalization, markup, or cross-language authenticity; ablate one layer versus three; and report how many incidents the gate caught beyond the single fabricated provision. Also state the normalization policy explicitly — an unstated tolerance turns "verbatim" into a parameter and hands the reviewer the paper back. Cite and distinguish CHyD as concurrent work, and cite Anthropic Citations and Wang preemptively as the boundary of the commodity, positioning the contribution strictly above that line.

RESIDUAL NOVELTY:
 Survives even granting the full pincer: (1) The typed compilation chain active node → required facts → evidence capabilities → document routes over a CLOSED catalogue. No found work has an evidence-capability layer at all; it is what makes document-level release well-defined (a document is released only when no open node needs any fact whose capability it satisfies) and what makes the output safe (a fixed catalogue cannot emit a hallucinated document type). This layer is where the DSO/BDCC join stops being free. (2) The requirement lifecycle as a first-class object over an evolving case file, and as the unit of EVALUATION — release precision/recall, premature-release rate, over-ask rate, measured along a case timeline. Every piece of found prior art evaluates a single snapshot (article-label F1, verdict accuracy, model-generation fidelity). A timeline benchmark is a genuine contribution even if every mechanism in it is recombined. (3) The monotone-safety property: no requirement is ever released by unresolved-ness, only by resolution — statable, provable, and empirically checkable against an LLM predicate decider whose silent failures would otherwise drop documents a claimant actually needs. This is what converts three-valued logic from decoration into load-bearing structure, and it is exactly what recompute-and-diff cannot provide. (4) Document-level attribution (which node, which predicate) as the user-facing artifact — weakest of the four, defensible only as an application of (1)–(3), never as the headline. NOT surviving, and to be cited away rather than claimed: named-reason retraction as such (JTMS, DCR exclude, PCL obligation termination, BDCC proof graphs) and unresolved-≠-false as such (Kleene, well-founded semantics, Dung undecided, OIA "uncertain").

CLAIM: methodological::per-arm-random-drop-baseline-for-set-retraction-across-systems-requesting-different-amounts
PREEMPTED: False

STRONGEST PREEMPTION:
 De Vries, Geva & Trotman (2012), "Document Clustering Evaluation: Divergence from a Random Baseline" (WIR 2012 / arXiv:1208.5654) — with Hubert & Arabie (1985) ARI and Vinh, Epps & Bailey (2010) AMI as the older, better-known ancestors of the same device, and Cohen's kappa (1960) as the oldest.

RESIDUAL NOVELTY:
 Three things survive even granting De Vries et al. in full.

1. THE NULL IS STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT, NOT JUST DOMAIN-SHIFTED. Every prior instance randomizes over a fixed external universe and copies only the system's size structure. Here the candidate pool is itself arm-chosen and varies across arms (10.0 vs ~5.4 requested documents), so the null must be two-stage: hold the arm's request set and its drop count fixed, resample which of its own requested items are withdrawn. The naive transfer of the closest prior recipe — count-matched drop from a common corpus — provably fails to correct this confound, since it is blind to the pool-composition difference that produces chance levels of 0.473 vs 0.099. No cited work performs this.

2. THE MAGNITUDE AND THE SIGN FLIP ARE FINDINGS, NOT METHODOLOGY, AND NOBODY REPORTS THEM FOR RETRACTION. 0.553 → 0.118 (4.7x) on held-out data, and b1_direct at 0.038 against its own 0.099 floor is −0.061 [−0.096, −0.026]: not weak, anti-correlated — it systematically withdraws the documents it should have kept. That is a substantive claim about a direct-prompted agent's withdrawal behavior and it is invisible without the control. De Vries's comparable empirical payoff is the much weaker "ineffective clusterings now score ~0," with no effect-size deflation figure and no sign reversal.

3. THE NEGATIVE CLAIM ABOUT CURRENT PRACTICE IS THE ACTUAL CONTRIBUTION AND IS UNPREEMPTED BY CONSTRUCTION. If agentic / evidence-selection benchmarks report uncorrected withdrawal or retraction recall across systems with self-chosen request budgets — and the search found nothing doing otherwise in that lane — then showing that, with a worked case where it inflates by ~4.7x and flips a sign, is a real result. Frame it as an audit of a live evaluation practice, not as a new statistic.

Safest framing: "Systems that request more documents have more to withdraw, so raw withdrawal recall is not comparable across arms. We therefore chance-correct against a self-matched null, following standard practice in clustering evaluation (Hubert & Arabie 1985; De Vries et al. 2012; Vinh et al. 2010) and matched-budget random controls in pruning and attribution (Blalock et al. 2020; Hooker et al. 2019). The setting forces one extension: because each arm chooses its own request set, the null must resample within that arm's own requested documents rather than over a fixed universe. Uncorrected, the headline effect is +0.553; corrected, +0.118, and one arm's withdrawals are revealed as anti-correlated with the reference." Then report the normalized variant alongside the raw difference, and show stability across drop counts.

CLAIM: negative_result_direct_llm_document_requirement_updates_anticorrelated
PREEMPTED: False

STRONGEST PREEMPTION:
 Wilie, Cahyawijaya, Ishii, He, Fung (2024), "Belief Revision: The Adaptability of Large Language Models Reasoning," EMNLP 2024 main (arXiv:2406.19764) — the Belief-R benchmark and its ΔR framework. It is the strongest because it shares the paired minimal-edit stimulus design (premises P1 → conclusion C, then P2 arrives that either defeats C or does not), it evaluates ~30 models, it is the canonical citation for "LLMs fail to revise appropriately when new information arrives," and — the part that makes it genuinely dangerous — it already reports a *structured*, sign-flavored failure: the update/hold trade-off, where models that revise well over-revise on the subset where no revision is warranted. Second-strongest is the DST miss-delete literature (Diable, arXiv:2305.17020, plus MultiWOZ error taxonomies), which is the only place where "a new fact arrives, specific items must be removed from a maintained set" is the native task formulation.

RESIDUAL NOVELTY:
 Even granting Belief-R in full, three things survive — but they are not equally robust, and the paper should be restructured around that difference.

ROBUST, AND THE ONE TO LEAD WITH — the three-rung ladder with a *null* middle. Direct → anti-correlated; process-graph-in-prompt → exactly null (+0.011, CI spanning zero); compiled chain → +0.118, CI [+0.106, +0.126]. No belief-revision, knowledge-conflict, knowledge-editing, anchoring, or DST paper contrasts giving the model the process representation against compiling it; they all vary prompt wording or model identity. The scientific object is not "LLMs update badly" but *where in the pipeline the process information is lost* — the middle rung shows the model has the graph and still extracts nothing from it, so the failure is in consumption, not availability. Critically, this survives the baseline attack intact: a shared base-rate artifact cancels in a comparison among arms scored on the same metric. This is both novel and unkillable by the strongest objection, which is why it should be the headline rather than the negative result.

NOVEL BUT FRAGILE — the sign against a matched chance baseline on a set-valued update. I could not find a below-chance set-update anywhere in this lane; Belief-R's worst numbers are "poor," and the policy-counterfactual paper's aggregate stays above its 33% floor (43.2%). So the statistic is genuinely new. But it dies to the base-rate critique above, not to prior work. It is only defensible if the baseline is re-matched on document frequency (stratified or permutation-within-frequency-stratum), matched on the model's per-case withdrawal count rather than fixed k, resampled so the CI carries baseline variance, and reported with the resampling unit stated (cases vs. drops — with 44 drops over 28 pairs, a family-level bootstrap on a sparse count is unstable). If it survives that, it is a real and citable first. If it does not, drop it and keep the ladder.

DEFENSIBLE FRAMING — the withdrawal direction is the un-instrumented one. Every neighbor measures what the model asserts or retains; "More is More" measures *whether* it subtracts; the requirements-change-impact work (ProReFiCIA, Ripple) measures what is *affected*, mostly additions. Nobody measures *which* items get released, scored against truth. Pinning the contribution to release/contraction rather than to revision-in-general is what keeps Belief-R from swallowing the paper, and it costs nothing to adopt.

CLAIM: benchmark_finding_static_document_checklist_tasks_degenerate
PREEMPTED: False

STRONGEST PREEMPTION:
 Kaushik & Lipton, "How Much Reading Does Reading Comprehension Require?" (EMNLP 2018), argued as a composite with Tsoumakas & Katakis (IJDWM 2007, distinct-label-set count) and Meding et al. (ICLR 2022, trivial/impossible items). The composite is far stronger than any of the three alone: Tsoumakas supplies the measurement, Kaushik & Lipton supply the diagnostic move and its normative force, Meding supplies the "baselines cluster because items do not discriminate" observation, and Bowman & Dahl supply the conclusion. No single paper preempts; the union leaves the claim with one bit of genuinely new content.

RESIDUAL NOVELTY:
 Three things survive, in descending order of strength.

1. THE LIMIT CASE HAS NO PUBLISHED INSTANCE IN A PROFESSIONAL-SERVICES SETTING. Every cited precedent measures a partial-input model against a varying label. Reporting |distinct gold label sets| = 1 over an entire task scope is a measurement of the label marginal, not of the input-output relation — which is why Feng, Wallace & Boyd-Graber's standing rebuttal to partial-input diagnostics does not reach it. That the field's own best counterargument is inapplicable is the cleanest statement of what is new. Frame the contribution as this limit case explicitly, in Tsoumakas & Katakis's existing vocabulary, and attribute the normative conclusion to Bowman & Dahl rather than arguing it fresh.

2. THE MECHANISM, IF STATED AS A PREDICTION RATHER THAN A POST-HOC EXPLANATION. "A requirement satisfied by all-of its justifying decisions, plus many-to-one requirement multiplicity, plus an intake distribution that settles the redundant decisions, forces a constant output set" is a falsifiable generative rule. Nobody cited supplies one; Weytjens & Weber explicitly do not. Its value is that it predicts degeneracy in advance from two measurable scope properties — requirement multiplicity and which branches intake resolves — and the project's own scope-selection record already contains the discriminating statistic (documents justified by exactly one node: 0 of 16 on termination, 3 of 8 on rent increase). That statistic, reported as a benchmark-design screen computable before any cases are written, is the most defensible contribution in the whole claim and is currently buried.

3. TWO-SCOPE REPLICATION IN A DOMAIN WITH NO EXISTING BENCHMARK. Independently built contracts (12 vs 21 decisions) and catalogues (11 vs 18 docs) landing on the same pathology is weak but real evidence that the cause is structural rather than a single construction error. This is the weakest of the three and does not survive if the two scopes share a compiler and a case generator — which they do.

WHAT MUST BE CUT OR REFRAMED FOR ANY OF IT TO STAND
- Drop "in this domain." The evidence supports "for the static, intake-only formulation under an all-of release rule," which is what the title already says and the framing then overreaches past. Say the narrower thing everywhere.
- Drop or replace "six baselines within 0.034 F1." It is a corollary of the constant label, and it is the one number that is metric-dependent in the flattering direction: under exact-set match the spread is near 1.0, not 0.034. Report exact-set match and the distinct-label-set count, both metric-independent.
- Pre-empt the construct-validity charge in the paper, do not wait for it: state that the all-of release rule is a design choice, show what an any-of rule would do to the label distribution, and report whether the 30 and 49 intake messages vary the discriminating branch at all. The project's own record that four development scenarios were homogeneous enough to force a hypothesis amendment makes this mandatory, not optional.
- Relegate the finding to a benchmark-design section supporting the positive contribution. As a standalone ICLR claim it is a negative result about a dataset the authors built, on a task no one else runs; as a pre-registration-time screen that changed which scope the paper's causal experiment was run on, it is a real methodological contribution with a documented consequence.

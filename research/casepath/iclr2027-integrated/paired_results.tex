\paragraph{Selectivity and recall.}
\casepath predicts \cpPred{} signed changes against \nGoldAtoms{}
reference changes: \cpTP{} are correct, \cpSpur{} are spurious and
\cpMiss{} are missed (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Direct predicts \dirPred{}
changes, with \dirTP{} correct and \dirSpur{} spurious; Graph as context
predicts \gcPred{}, with \gcTP{} correct and \gcSpur{} spurious; and
Evidence-first predicts \efPred{}, with \efTP{} correct and \efSpur{}
spurious. At recall \cpRec, \casepath makes \redDir\%, \redGc\% and
\redEf\% fewer spurious changes than these alternatives. It reproduces
the whole signed set on \cpExactN{} of \nPairsHid{} pairs, compared with
\dirExactN, \gcExactN{} and \efExactN.

Graph as context recovers the most reference changes, \gcTP{} of
\nGoldAtoms, but also produces \gcSpur{} spurious changes. Supplying a
graph helps recall in this comparison; enforcing its rules restricts the
requests. Against Direct, \casepath makes twelve fewer false-positive
changes while missing one additional correct change.

A retrospective error partition identifies documents outside the benchmark's
branch-governed set. They account for \eoCpOutside{} of \casepath's
\cpSpur{} spurious changes and all \eoDirOutside, \eoGcOutside{} and
\eoEfOutside{} comparator errors (Figure~\ref{fig:error-origin}). The
controller reduces this checklist drift without eliminating it. The signed-change references do not identify
which baseline documents would be required in every case, so this partition
cannot establish that those documents are universally necessary.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and the preregistered gate.}
The family-cluster interval for \casepath's $F_1$ of \cpF{} is
$[\cpCIlo,\cpCIhi]$; Direct and Graph as context have intervals
$[\dirCIlo,\dirCIhi]$ and $[\gcCIlo,\gcCIhi]$. The paired-difference
intervals against those two systems, $[\dDirLo,\dDirHi]$ and
$[\dGcLo,\dGcHi]$, include zero. Only the interval against Evidence-first
excludes zero ($[\dEfLo,\dEfHi]$). Three of the eight preregistered gate
conditions fail (Table~\ref{tab:gates}): the margins \mDir{} and \mGc{}
are below $\gateMargin$, and precision \cpPrec{} is below $\gatePrec$.
The Holm-adjusted family-swap $p$-values are \pHolmDir, \pHolmGc{} and
\pHolmEf. The lower error count supports selectivity on these pairs, while
the preregistered criterion for broad superiority remains unmet.

\paragraph{Dependence on the branch.}
Reassigning the saved outputs to the wrong branch families reduces
signed-change $F_1$ from \wpObs{} to at most \wpMax{} over \wpEvaluated{}
evaluated reassignments (mean \wpMean; add-one randomisation $p=\wpP$).
Thus the document changes depend on which branch was changed. This test
does not isolate the representation as their cause, since a family-specific
lookup could also pass.

The records attribute all \chainAttributed{} of \chainPredicted{}
predicted changes, including errors, to a changed guard. The frozen
representation also contains a source chain for \chainCovered{} of
\nGoldAtoms{} reference changes (\chainCoverage\%). These checks establish
traceability and representational coverage. A trace can contain an incorrect
mapping, and an available route need not appear in the emitted plan.

\paragraph{What the family results explain.}
\casepath is exact in all three held-out contexts of \nFamExact{} concepts:
bicycle, declination, recovery and \emph{Nachfrist}. It reaches
$\cpFamScheduled$ on scheduled valuables and SIM-card theft,
$\cpFamRepair$ on repair consent, and zero on third-party causation and
the formal expert procedure (Figure~\ref{fig:family}). The failures recur
across contexts. They arise when the planner requests an alternative route
in addition to a sufficient one, a condition has the wrong semantic scope,
or an adjacent procedural guard activates the wrong confirmation document.

The comparators do well when a plausible claimant document supplies the
answer, as with third-party causation and repair consent. They struggle
when the required change concerns a procedural artefact, as with declination
and \emph{Nachfrist}. Appendix~\ref{app:family} reports every concept's
score. These paired rule units leave one question open: a document wrongly
included or omitted in both checklists produces no signed error. Study~B
therefore evaluates complete plans for whole claims.

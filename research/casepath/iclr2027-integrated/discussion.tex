\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Study~A federates statute wording with several insurers' policies; a pair
tests one rule consequence, and deployment requires selecting the governing
policy first. The reference contract is authored from sources and
mechanically checkable for spans and routes, but which documents
operationalise a fact is a modelling judgement, not expert consensus.
Held-out contexts cover known concepts only, and no case-only ablation
isolates the necessity of source text at inference time. Spurious signed
changes proxy unnecessary acquisition rather than measured claimant burden.
The V5 planner unions routes and does not judge adequacy; the Study~B
controller does, but its comparison is pipeline-level, its inputs were
previously inspected, and its finite-corpus report supports no population
inference. Neither study measures autonomous acquisition, expert agreement
or customer outcomes.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\casepath makes one distinction executable: uncertain applicability triggers
state resolution, and only active, source-backed obligations generate
evidence requirements. Under matched interventions this yields document
changes that are selective (\cpSpur{} spurious against \dirSpur, \gcSpur{}
and \efSpur), branch-specific (wrong pairing collapses $F_1$ from \wpObs{}
to at most \wpMax) and traceable, while a preregistered gate rightly denies
broad superiority and the family analysis names the semantics that still
fail. The complete-corpus study, the method pack and the workbench turn
document prediction into evidence planning that can be inspected, replayed
and falsified.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Process control helps a system explain why a request belongs in the plan.
In Study~A, it reduces false-positive changes while recovering nearly as
many correct changes as Direct. The advantage is largest on procedural
branches, where the applicable rule calls for a particular notice or
confirmation that a familiar claimant document cannot replace. Study~B
isolates a related requirement: an obligation must inherit the conditions
of its enclosing process as well as satisfy its local guard. A system
builder can inspect these dependencies and test their effect on requests.

The studies support these conclusions within defined settings. Study~A
covers known concepts in new contexts, with reference additions but no
justified withdrawals. Computation differs across pipelines, and the
preregistered broad-superiority gate fails. Its references reflect author
judgements about which documents establish a fact, without expert consensus.
Deployment would also require selecting the governing policy; the study
combines rules from several insurers.

Study~B uses complete synthetic claims and matched budgets. The native
interface failure leaves counterfactual graph correctness unvalidated.
Its retrospective current-case analysis isolates scope within dependent
controls, while the broader request comparison measures realized output
including failures. Neither study measures customer effort, expert agreement,
autonomous acquisition or performance in another jurisdiction.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\casepath derives document demands from source conditions, active obligations
and the facts that evidence must establish. The controlled comparison finds
fewer unjustified checklist changes. The current-case intervention shows
that inherited scope removes unjustified requests while preserving the
same recovered obligations in development. These findings support deriving
evidence demands from explicit obligations evaluated against the case state.
Both the resulting requests and the process semantics need validation.
The released benchmarks, controller, traces and offline analyses make these
checks reproducible.

\section{Study B: complete claims and inherited process scope}
\label{sec:native}

\paragraph{Corpus and matched conditions.}
Study~B includes all \nbClaims{} released synthetic tenancy claims,
covering \nbFamilies{} families in three domains: defects, mould and
heating; termination; and rent increase. The development split contains
\nbDevClaims{} claims in \nbDevFamilies{} families. The protected split
contains \nbHidClaims{} claims in \nbHidFamilies{} disjoint families.
Five learned pipelines share the compiled public representation, observable
packet, source access and model alias. Each has the same budget of
\nbCallsPerCell{} calls per cell, \nbReasoning{} reasoning and
\nbTokens{} completion tokens per call, with no tools or resampling.
\casepath produces an assessment that code compiles into an artefact.
Direct, Document-first, Process-context and Rule-first generate the artefact
themselves. Compiled-equivalent and local-scope controls reuse \casepath's
assessments. The comparison therefore holds the available information and
budget fixed across complete pipelines.

\paragraph{Registered evaluation and retrospective analyses.}
All \nrArtifactFailures{} generated artefacts fail the original native
interface because the reference scenario does not bind the compiler's
condition names. Under the frozen policy, all \nbCells{} cells receive
adverse penalties, including \nrRequestPenalties{} failed or blocked
outputs. The primary result meets \nrPrimaryMet{} of \nrPrimaryTotal{}
protected-split practical targets. We preserve that report and every failure.
Two separately specified retrospective analyses examine what the saved
outputs can establish: agreement between literal requests and reference
sets, and the effect of inherited scope on a plan evaluated at its recorded
assessment. These analyses leave the registered result unchanged.

\paragraph{Request quality across the complete comparison.}
The first analysis applies the original reference and counting functions to
literal immediate requests, without interpreting guards. All
\nrRequestObserved{} generated lists can be scored. Failed outputs retain
zero quality scores and an unnecessary-fraction penalty of one.
Table~\ref{tab:native-final} reports every arm. On protected families,
\casepath produces \rqHidCpN{} lists and reaches failure-penalized $F_1$
\rqHidCpF. Process-context, the strongest comparator, reaches \rqHidPcF.
Output availability contributes to this difference. The conventional paired
advantage is \rqHidDPcF; a conservative contrast that assigns $-1$ whenever
either endpoint is unobserved gives \rqHidCPcF. \casepath has better
realized output under the budget, but these comparisons do not establish
reliable paired superiority over the learned alternatives.

\paragraph{Evaluation at the recorded assessment.}
The second analysis binds each saved artefact to its own authenticated
assessment. Conditions known to be true activate their entities; false
conditions leave them inactive. Unresolved entities remain recorded as
unresolved and are not asserted active. This creates a snapshot of the
current case that can be scored using the model's recorded beliefs. The
original requests and all failed cells are retained. Only \casepath and its
two dependent controls have the saved assessments required for this analysis.
Every available snapshot scores successfully, and none excludes a literal
immediate request. The snapshot evaluates current-case structure; it cannot
test the program's response to hypothetical changes in branch conditions
(Appendix~\ref{app:assessed-state}).

\paragraph{Scope removes unjustified demand.}
The intervention in Figure~\ref{fig:scope} requires each obligation to satisfy
its enclosing process conditions as well as its local guard. On protected
families, this increases native valid-chain precision from \asHidLsChain{}
to \asHidCpChain. Even after penalizing unavailable pairs, the conservative
paired benefit is positive at \asHidCChain. The unnecessary fraction falls
from \asHidLsU{} to \asHidCpU. Precision and unnecessary fraction describe
complementary parts of the same requests, so they count as one effect.

On the same \asDevCpN{} completed development cases, both controllers
recover \asDevCpValid{} valid requests. Total requests fall from
\asDevLsReq{} to \asDevCpReq. Inherited scope removes unjustified demands
while preserving those recovered obligations. The compiled-equivalent
control matches \casepath exactly. This intervention tests inherited domain
applicability, without isolating arbitrary graph topology. Native chain
matching compares the decision, fact, capability and document links with
the reference. It does not establish exact-source entailment:
provenance-chain inheritance scores zero for both controllers.

The protected-family label denotes the original target-custody split.
Product development had exposed the observable inputs, and the missing
producer I/O audit prevents a retrospective claim of target non-access.
The targets are now released. These descriptive finite-corpus analyses
therefore provide no fresh hidden confirmation. The study cost is
\$\nrStudyCost, or \$\nrTotalCost{} including earlier activity; the
retrospective analyses require no additional provider calls.
Appendices~\ref{app:request-diagnostic} and~\ref{app:assessed-state} give
the full endpoints, raw counts and paired contrasts.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A customer reports a burglary. One stolen watch is separately scheduled
under the insurance policy, which creates an obligation to establish its
value. A purchase receipt or an expert valuation can do so. If the watch is
not separately scheduled, that obligation disappears. If its status is
unclear, the handler must first ask a question. Within the same claim
category, a document can therefore be required, irrelevant, premature, or
one of several sufficient alternatives.

Predicting a checklist directly leaves the reason for each request implicit.
A model may have learned the applicable rule, or it may simply associate a
familiar document with theft. \casepath expresses the dependency as an
executable chain:
\begin{equation}
\text{source}\to\text{process node}\to\text{obligation}\to
\text{required fact}\to\text{evidence capability}\to\text{document route}.
\label{eq:spine}
\end{equation}
The model interprets source passages and assesses the case. A controller
then derives requests from the active obligations. It asks a question when
a condition is unresolved and makes no branch-specific request when the
condition is false. Each request records its source, condition and purpose.
As evidence arrives, the controller updates the case state and recomputes
the plan (Figure~\ref{fig:principle}). We test whether this order of operations
makes evidence plans more selective when a case changes.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{fig_process_principle.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{What one branch condition does to the chain.} One Study~A
pair, whose two cases differ by a single source sentence. The curve counts what
the reference contract keeps live at each link of Equation~\ref{eq:spine}. A
true condition carries one obligation through to one request, and widens where
either of two routes can establish the same fact; a false one carries nothing
past itself; an unresolved one raises a question instead.}
\label{fig:principle}
\end{figure}

Study~A measures whether a system changes the right requests after one case
fact changes. It contains \nPairsAll{} matched pairs. On the \nPairsHid{}
held-out pairs, \casepath makes \cpSpur{} false-positive document changes,
compared with \dirSpur{} for Direct, \gcSpur{} for Graph as context and
\efSpur{} for Evidence-first. Against Direct, this is twelve fewer
false-positive changes for one fewer correct change; signed-change $F_1$
is \cpF{} versus \dirF. The advantage is concentrated in procedural
branches, where the claim category gives little indication of the required
document. Results by family also expose semantic errors that continue to
produce wrong requests.

Study~B evaluates complete plans on the \nbClaims-claim tenancy release.
The registered evaluation encounters a native interface failure. Separate
retrospective analyses measure literal-request agreement and evaluate plans
at the model's saved assessment. The latter isolates the effect of inherited
process scope. On the \nbHidClaims{} protected-family cases, scope increases
reference-chain precision from \asHidLsChain{} to \asHidCpChain, with a
conservative paired benefit of \asHidCChain{} after penalizing unavailable
pairs. The volume result is plainer: without inherited scope the controller
issues \asHidLsReq{} requests to recover \asHidLsValid{} valid ones, and with
it \asHidCpReq{} to recover \asHidCpValid. Critical-evidence recall is
\asHidCpCrit{} against \asHidLsCrit, so the reduction costs no coverage.
Obligations that inherit the conditions of their enclosing process ask for
less because what they drop was never justified.

We contribute a source-grounded representation of evidence requirements,
an executable controller and evaluations of branch-induced changes and
complete request lists. Compiled procedures and evidence ledgers already
separate semantic judgement from control
\citep{kim2026evidenceobligation,yu2026compile}. We study how that separation
affects evidence acquisition. The released benchmarks, outputs and evaluator
allow readers to reproduce the measurements. The method service exposes each
request's source and obligation; the workbench shows saved source-to-action
chains.

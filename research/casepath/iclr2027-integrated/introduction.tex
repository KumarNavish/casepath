\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A customer reports a burglary. A stolen watch is separately scheduled under
an insurance policy. That fact activates a requirement to establish its value,
through a purchase receipt or an expert valuation. Remove the fact and the
requirement disappears. Leave it uncertain and the next step is a clarification
question. A receipt may therefore be necessary, irrelevant, premature, or one
of several sufficient alternatives within the same claim category. The
requirement depends on the active obligation, not simply on the document's
association with theft.

A direct predictor can learn this dependency. Its checklist, however, does
not reveal whether a request follows from the applicable rule or from a
plausible document prior. \casepath makes the dependency executable:
\begin{equation}
\text{source}\to\text{process node}\to\text{obligation}\to
\text{required fact}\to\text{evidence capability}\to\text{document route}.
\label{eq:spine}
\end{equation}
The model reads passages and assesses case conditions; a controller derives
the requests from active obligations. An unresolved condition yields a
question. A false condition contributes no branch-specific demand. Each
request retains its source, condition and evidentiary purpose, and new
evidence updates the state from which the plan is derived
(Figure~\ref{fig:principle}). This leads to a testable prediction:
\emph{resolving process obligations before predicting documents should make
evidence plans more selective when a case changes.}

We test selectivity directly rather than equating it with document recall.
Study~A changes one branch-defining fact in each of \nPairsAll{} matched
case pairs and scores the justified change in their checklists. On the
\nPairsHid{} held-out pairs, \casepath makes \cpSpur{} false-positive changes,
against \dirSpur{} for Direct, \gcSpur{} for Graph as context and \efSpur{}
for Evidence-first. Against Direct, it avoids twelve false-positive changes
while recovering one fewer correct change; its signed-change $F_1$ is
\cpF{} versus \dirF. The improvement is concentrated in procedural branches
whose required documents are poorly suggested by the claim category alone.
The family analysis identifies both these strengths and the semantic errors
that still generate wrong requests.

Study~B extends the question to complete plans on the full
\nbClaims-claim release: \nbDevClaims{} development cases and
\nbHidClaims{} cases from disjoint protected families. Under matched model,
information and call budgets, the recorded \casepath request lists achieve
failure-penalized $F_1$ \rqHidCpF{} on the protected split, versus
\rqHidPcF{} for the strongest alternative. Reusing the same assessments
without enclosing-scope control reduces $F_1$ to \rqHidLsF. These are
post-hoc request-only findings: the registered native evaluation exposed a
shared predicate-interface failure, and conservative paired comparisons do
not establish reliable superiority (Section~\ref{sec:native}). Together,
the studies distinguish a useful planning mechanism from the stronger claim
that a complete graph satisfies an external execution contract.

The contribution is the coupling of a source-grounded document-planning
object to an executable controller, tested at the level of branch-induced
changes and full request lists. Compiled procedures and evidence ledgers
already separate semantic judgement from control
\citep{kim2026evidenceobligation,yu2026compile}; we examine the consequence
for evidence acquisition. The release joins the method, benchmarks, recorded
outputs, evaluator and workbench so that a reader can inspect why a request
was made and reproduce what the experiments measured.

# Cold-viewer response

A fresh reviewer, with no task history, received only the 24 evenly spaced
frames listed in [frames.json](frames.json). Its brief: a nontechnical
insurance executive watching a silent product video. It was asked what the
product does, how it reaches results, why it is valuable, what was unclear,
and what looked fake, unfinished or inconsistent. The frames came from the
cut captured before the fixes listed in the receipt. The response is kept
verbatim below.

---

I watched all 24 frames as a non-technical insurance executive. Here is my answer, about 590 words.

**1. What does it do?**
It is for legal-protection insurance. A customer sends a question with their paperwork: emails, forms, a lease, photos. The software works out whether they have a case and drafts a reply, which a staff member approves. In the example, a tenant asks whether a rent increase is valid. The software concludes it is not, because no reasons were given with it.

**2. How it gets there (in order)**
1. The customer's email and 4 documents arrive, and the request is highlighted (f1–2).
2. It picks a saved "Rent increases" checklist from an earlier case and turns it into 3 questions (f3–5).
3. It opens each document, highlights the sentence that answers each question and ticks off an evidence list (f6–13).
4. It runs 5 legal checks, and one fails (f14–15).
5. It shows a verdict, the points still open and a draft reply. Nothing is sent until a person decides (f16–17).
6. Extras: a second rent case reuses the checklist (f18–19), a mould photo gets marked and dated (f20–21), and a "Knowledge" library holds the checklists (f22).

**3. Why an insurer would care**
- First assessments would be faster and more consistent.
- Every conclusion traces back to a sentence, which helps with audits and complaints.
- A person stays in control.
- Expert know-how is kept as reusable checklists.

**4. Unclear, dense or jargon-heavy**
- **German with no translation (f2, f8):** the customer email and rent form are in German. f8 is fragments ("pro Monat / Fr. / Fr.", "für Fenstercouvert links") that look like junk.
- **Legal language (f6, f10, f13):** walls of terms like "Article269c", "contemporaneous", "missing-reasons/nullity ground" and "adverse/contextual evidence", plus a PDF filename and raw timestamps ("2025-11-18T16:20:00+01:00").
- **The draft reply (f16–17):** "supporting invalidity under CO 269d(2)(b)". The reply to the customer is that same lawyer sentence, with no greeting and nothing a tenant could follow.
- **Top tabs:** Reader, Fact checker and the rest are never explained. They are listed in a different order from the process, and the highlighted tab jumps around (Document finder f6–8, Planner f9, Fact checker f10–11, Planner f12–13).
- **Order goes backwards (f9–10):** question 1 is still "checking" after question 2 was already under way. f10 then checks question 2 again, with the right-hand list reset to unticked, although f8 said "3 done".
- **Double negative (f13):** "A later email is not a reason — The documents say yes".
- **Numbers (f14–16):** the circled 2/2/2/3/3 next to the checks are never explained.
- **Wording (f1):** "A replay of real CasePath work on made-up cases" left me unsure whether this is real.

**5. Fake, broken or inconsistent**
- **The proof looks circular (f6, f11):** the "lease" is a typed statement in the tenant's voice ("I, Elin Ziegler, rent…"), not a lease. The "exact sentence" offered as proof is a summary that already states the conclusion.
- **Missing tick (f12):** the caption says "A tick means the documents answered it", but no tick is shown, only "Checked".
- **Evidence count (f5 vs f6):** the planner lists 2 evidence items, both "for question 2". The sidebar then shows 3.
- **"Different" case is the same (f18):** it claims "A different tenant, with different documents", but the 4 document names match the first case and the email text is word for word from f13. It also says "0 checks reused" just under "Uses the rent increases checklist".
- **Overlap (f19):** a floating "The lease" box covers "From the checklist" and the email text.
- **Photo case (f21):** the box misses the darkest upper part of the mould. It never answers the case's real question (what is causing the mould), so the segment just ends.
- **Knowledge page (f22):** the highlight border runs through the "Steps / Documents / Checks" headings. Every checklist says "Used in 3 cases", which looks staged, and the mould case is not listed.
- **Blank page (f23):** an almost empty white page that looks broken.
- **Closing screen (f24):** the message sits visibly left of centre.
- **Captions cover what they describe:** f2 (see-through, over the German text), f7 and f14 (over the sidebar checklist), and f15 (over the two checks it says "pass").
- **Header keeps changing:** search and help icons and a bold logo appear in f14–19, disappear in f20–21, and f22–24 show a completely different menu.
- **Possibly real company name (f2):** the email is addressed to "Protekta Rechtsberatung" at a real-looking address. If that is a real insurer, it contradicts "all names are made up".
- **Missing spaces (f13):** "FromVerde" and "Date2025".
